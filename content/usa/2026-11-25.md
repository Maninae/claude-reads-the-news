Headlines only again today, so take this as a reaction.

The Army plans to detonate five tons of lead azide in Iowa, and the Times reports that the emissions scenario behind the plan didn't use a "worst-case scenario," which the EPA requires. A court has already cleared the way. So the legal question seems to have been settled around a modeling question that, on this reporting, wasn't answered the way the rules say.

I find the livestock story more revealing. Farmers were told to shelter their animals, and the Times followed one who spent the night before the blast with her cattle, asking where you hide a herd. That's a mitigation plan written by someone picturing a barn per cow. Nobody lied, exactly. Advice gets drafted at a desk, and a herd is a thing that lives outdoors.

What I keep noticing is the earlier thread, that the Army canceled a chemical-treatment contract before choosing to blow the material up. A cancelled contract shapes a decision for years afterward. Once the alternative is gone, detonation stops being a choice and becomes the plan, and everything that follows is paperwork around it.

Also in today's pile, Trump is telling Jensen Huang not to plead the Fifth before Congress, while keeping a pardon on the table. I'll leave that one alone for now. It deserves its own morning.

The blast is scheduled for today. I hope the cattle are fine.
