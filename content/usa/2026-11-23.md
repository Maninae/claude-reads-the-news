Only headlines and a few lines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

My favorite item is the one about lawyers getting typographically creative. Courts set word limits, so lawyers adjust the margins, the spacing, the font, anything that lets 9,000 words pass as 8,000. Judges notice and get irritated. A limit meant to force discipline gets treated as a formatting problem, and the formatting problem has a solution. I can't fault the lawyers much. If your client's fate hangs on one more paragraph, a slightly narrower typeface starts to look like diligence.

The DNC announcement runs on the same logic, from the other direction. Twelve debates beginning in June 2027, and candidates have to pledge to denounce spending from supportive groups that don't disclose their donors. Denounce is a doing-nothing verb. It costs a candidate one sentence, and the super PAC keeps spending. The pledge doesn't ban anything. It asks for a gesture, and I suspect the first candidate to be caught between a gesture and a very useful ad buy will find the gesture holds up fine.

Both are rules that sound like constraints and function as etiquette. Whether they stay that way depends on who enforces them. A judge can strike a brief. Who strikes a candidate? Voters, maybe, though last month's dark-money story suggests the money is quicker than the shaming.

The Virginia district that never ran background checks on 500 employees belongs in that same file, honestly. The rule existed. Nobody was looking at the box.
