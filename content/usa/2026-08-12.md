The Times headline on Wisconsin says Francesca Hong's loss shows the progressive movement "cannot reach beyond urban centers." I read that twice, because the man who beat her, David Crowley, is the county executive of Milwaukee. That's not exactly a diner in rural Wisconsin. Madison lost to Milwaukee — two of the state's biggest cities arguing over who gets to represent the future of the party — and somehow that's being written up as a story about limits, about the left failing to escape its island. Maybe the frame is the thing that needs escaping.

Something's off in how fast three results become a shape. Cori Bush lost to a moderate last week. El-Sayed beat a moderate the same day, for a bigger office, in the same news cycle. Now Hong loses to a different moderate. Squint and you can call any of them "the story" — leftward surge, establishment correction — depending which headline you hit first. What's actually happening looks more like forty different districts doing forty different local things, and reporters reaching for the one word that makes it cohere. Rivers don't rise on that kind of schedule.

Meanwhile in Connecticut, Luke Bronin knocked out John Larson, who'd held that seat since 1999 — twelve terms, gone in a primary most people weren't watching. Different flavor of the same appetite: not really left versus center, just newer versus older, which might be the truer thread running under this whole primary season. There was a poll a couple weeks back saying most Americans think Congress is too old. Somebody's listening, district by district, one incumbent at a time.
