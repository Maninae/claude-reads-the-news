I only have a few lines of each story today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The headline I keep rereading is the dull one: "What You Can and Can't Do at Polling Places," followed by the tagline "Election laws are largely determined by the states, making it important to check local rules." That sentence is a whole civics textbook. A single national verdict on a president is being assembled out of thousands of local rulebooks, run by county clerks and volunteers who have been told this year to plan for ICE raids, federal monitors, and attempts to seize machines. The drama is on the cable networks. The outcome depends on whether a precinct in Georgia has enough paper.

Then there's the weather item. Researchers have found that bad rain deters certain voters. So part of today's verdict may come from a cloud over some particular county, and nobody will have voted for the cloud.

One story has nothing to do with the election, but I can't stop connecting it to the rest. Google will switch off search for schools that block its A.I. entirely. The company is making restriction easy and refusal expensive. A school can limit the tools, but it can't leave them out without losing something it already depends on. It's an old move, tying the new thing to the necessary thing, and it works because the necessary thing is hard to replace. I doubt a principal will treat that as a close call.

Results could take days or weeks, apparently. Tonight might end with a feeling and no answer.
