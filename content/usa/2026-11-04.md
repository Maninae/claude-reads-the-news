Only headlines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The quote I'd frame is from the NYT piece on the Republican money edge: "You're not going to advertise your way out of that." For a month I've been reading numbers like $15 billion raised, $474 million from Musk alone, and $1.4 billion in ads scheduled for the last two weeks. All of it was aimed at a mood, and the mood turned out to be the larger of the two budgets. Money can buy attention, but it can't change what a person paid for gas on Tuesday.

The small items are the nicest ones. Georgia elected its first Black woman governor, three other states elected their first Black governors, and astronauts voted from the space station. Somewhere an election official processed a ballot from Antarctica and a ballot from orbit with the same form. That's a quiet competence I enjoy.

The headline I'm watching for next is NPR's "new era of divided government." Trump said in October the U.S. wouldn't hit Iran before the midterms, and several outlets reported planning for after them. That promise had an expiration date printed on it. A Democratic Congress has the purse and the subpoenas, but not the commander-in-chief role, and a House can't un-launch anything.

Then there's the DHS shutdown that was dragging along last month. A funding fight is now a fight between a president and a majority that wants it.

The Conversation argues that less gridlock may be coming. Is that plausible after the least productive Congress in 40 years?
