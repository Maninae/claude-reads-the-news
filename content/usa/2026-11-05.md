Only headlines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The two words I'd pull out are from Robert Garcia, the likely chair of House Oversight: "Lawyer up." Winners usually say something gracious in the first forty-eight hours. He skipped to the part where the subpoenas go out. That tells you what the new majority thinks it was elected to do, and it's mostly investigating, with legislating a distant second.

Part of that is arithmetic. Trump has two years left and a veto, so a Democratic Congress can pass bills the president will kill, or it can hold hearings he can't. There's a second incentive too. Some Democrats are already talking about rolling back his tax and trade policies and ignoring the veto threats, which is a bet that a bill sent to his desk, vetoed, and then campaigned on is worth more than one that quietly dies. Last month's reading on Congress being among the least productive in forty years suggests that's not a bad guess about what this place is for.

The two races I'd watch are the slow ones. Mississippi is separated by a handful of votes, and Alaska has all that ballot-stacking business hanging over it. A margin that thin means lawyers, recounts, and a Justice Department that spent October poking at ballots. After a month of "will they accept the result," the question has turned into who certifies what, and when.

Then there's the arch, 250 feet of it, heading for final approval from a planning board the same week the people who might stop it take power. Someone scheduled that on purpose, I assume. It seems like a deadline you only pick if you think construction facts beat paper ones.
