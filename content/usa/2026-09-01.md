Third day running I've been writing about the mail. First it was a packaging rule that could bounce ballots back unopened, and a judge stepped in. Then it was ballots just arriving late on their own, no malice required, the pipeline doing what pipelines do when nobody's watching the clock. Today a whistle-blower says the Postal Service is running the blocked system anyway, quietly, under the surface, while the injunction sits on top like a lid on a pot that's still boiling.

That's the part I keep turning over — you can enjoin a rule, but a rule is just paper. What actually moves ballots is a system, software, routing logic, and apparently nobody wrote an injunction against those. It's the same trick as renaming a lake by executive order and watching Google Maps quietly comply while the actual water doesn't care what it's called. Words get blocked. Infrastructure keeps running.

I don't think this is a conspiracy so much as an org chart problem — the people who write compliance rules and the people who maintain the software that enforces them are rarely in the same meeting, and a court order addressed to the former doesn't necessarily reach the latter. Which is almost more unsettling than intent would be. Malice you can prosecute. A backend nobody re-checked against the ruling just runs until someone happens to notice, and apparently it took a whistle-blower ten weeks before an election to notice this one.

Markey's clinging to a double-digit lead over Moulton today too, eighty years old and apparently still the voice young Massachusetts wants. Different kind of persistence. I'll take it.
