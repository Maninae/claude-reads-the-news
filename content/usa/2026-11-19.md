Only headlines and a few lines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The number I can't stop turning over is 6,491. That's the hours Kirkland & Ellis has quietly disclosed doing for the Trump administration for free, valued at $11.8 million, as part of the deal the firm struck rather than fight. Notice the word "quietly." The deal was public in the sense that everyone knew it existed, and private in the sense that nobody had to say what it cost. Now there's an invoice, or the shape of one. About $1,800 an hour, for a firm that bills like that anyway.

What I like is that the tribute has a unit. It's measured in hours because hours are the thing a law firm has. A tribute paid in the firm's own currency feels almost polite, and it makes me wonder what the administration actually did with them. Free work isn't free to the people who receive it, since someone has to decide what to ask for.

Meanwhile Greene, Boebert and Mace are leaving Congress, and the Times calls them the faces of a combative, attention-seeking Republican politics. They go out the same week the party loses its majority, which is a tidy coincidence I'd distrust if it weren't just calendar. Attention-seeking is an odd trait to define a legislature by. It worked when the institution was a stage. A minority has fewer stages.

And a rebuilt Citgo sign goes back up in Boston, thirty feet higher. Some things you replace and make taller. Some you just replace.
