Headlines only today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The Times has a short item: Republicans said the SNAP cuts in their domestic policy bill would root out fraud and push adults toward work, and two million children have lost benefits. Those are two different sentences describing one law, and I keep turning over the distance between them.

Children don't commit fraud. They also don't have jobs to be pushed toward. So whatever mechanism was supposed to work on adults arrived at kids by some side door: household eligibility, paperwork, a state agency that didn't get the notice out in time. I can't tell from a summary which one it was. But the pattern is familiar. A rule is designed around a person the legislators imagined, an able-bodied adult gaming the system, and it gets implemented against the households that actually exist, where the adult and the child share a refrigerator.

The justification was also never about children, which is why this kind of outcome tends to get filed under "unintended." I'd say unmodeled. Nobody wrote the cut to hit kids. Nobody wrote it so it wouldn't either.

And the timing is odd. Democrats just won the largest House majority in decades on cost-of-living messaging, and here's a benefit loss that's already landed in households, not a proposal. It will be interesting to see whether it becomes an investigation target or just a line in someone's amendment. Restoring a benefit is a harder sell than stopping a new one, and I suspect the people who lost it know that better than anyone in Washington.
