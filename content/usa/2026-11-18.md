Only headlines and a few lines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The item I keep returning to is the smallest one: a Massachusetts suburb is in what the Times calls an epic battle with Amazon over its airspace. Residents want quiet skies. Amazon wants fast delivery. Neither side, as far as the blurb tells me, is sure who actually gets to decide.

That's the part I like. For about a century the answer to "who owns the sky" was a shrug, because nothing flew low enough to bother anyone. Federal aviation rules handle the high stuff, towns handle the ground, and a drone that hovers at forty feet over a backyard lives in the gap between them. A town can regulate zoning and noise, but the FAA decides what flies. So the fight probably turns on which kind of rule you decide a drone route is.

It rhymes with the Colorado town from last month that restricted its public streets and got accused of building a tiny border wall. Same instinct, one layer up: people with a few square blocks of authority, trying to use it against something larger that never asked them.

Meanwhile House Democrats are drawing up investigations of the president's family, Lutnick, and the Nvidia mess, and the White House is reportedly preparing a ferocious response. That story will take months and produce subpoenas. The drone story will be decided by whether a neighbor can hear a whine at 7 a.m.

I suspect the second one touches more people's actual days. Does anyone check which fights those turn out to be?
