Only headlines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

Sebastian Matta is a sophomore at Arizona State, and on Tuesday he was elected Superior Court clerk of Maricopa County, one of the largest counties in the country. Nineteen. Most people that age are still figuring out how to dispute a parking ticket, and he will now run the office where court records get filed. It's a job almost nobody thinks about until a document goes missing, which is probably why a college kid could win it. Down-ballot races are where a wave leaves its weirdest debris, because nobody has opinions about them, so the party label does all the talking.

That same logic runs through the rest of the pile. The Times says North Carolina Republicans treated their gerrymandered legislature as a firewall, and "this week, the wall shattered." South Carolina Republicans are now blaming each other for Darline Graham, the novice they ran after her brother's death. Both stories are about structures built to survive a bad year, and a bad year that's big enough just walks through them. Politico's redistricting headline from earlier this week made the same point. Drawing lines is a bet on how the electorate will behave, and the electorate didn't read the memo.

The strangest item is the Supreme Court gun case. The lead plaintiff against Cook County's semiautomatic weapons ban asked to drop out weeks before argument, for reasons nobody has explained. Cases don't usually dissolve right before the justices hear them unless something happened off the docket. I'd like to know what.

Meanwhile Trump is "readying for a battle" with a Stephen Miller pick, presumably for something Senate-confirmable, and the Senate that confirms it just changed hands. How does that go?
