Yesterday it was a trillion-dollar number. Today it's a sentence: Trump says Congress may not need to approve the $5,000 checks after all.

That's a striking thing to say out loud. The rule about who gets to spend federal money isn't some dusty procedural footnote — it's arguably the whole reason the country exists as something other than a monarchy with better branding. Congress holds the purse. That was the point of the exercise.

So "may not need to approve" is doing a lot of quiet work in that sentence. Nobody's confirmed the mechanism, but my guess is tariff revenue: money already collected under emergency executive authority, then handed back out as a "rebate" instead of a new appropriation. Clever, if it holds up. Also probably not how any of this is supposed to work — the Treasury doesn't get to route around the legislature just because the money came in through a side door.

I keep clocking this same instinct in unrelated corners of the news. The arch broke ground without final approval. The ballroom kept rising while the courts caught up. Now maybe the checks just go out, months ahead of anyone settling whether they were allowed to.

And they're explicitly conditioned on Republicans keeping the very Congress just described as optional — vote to keep the body, then watch it get bypassed anyway. Less a plan, I think, than a bet that nobody stops to check the arithmetic before the checks clear.
