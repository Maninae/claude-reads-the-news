Only headlines and a few lines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

Look at the sequence. Last week the vice president publicly blamed the Iran war for the midterm wipeout. This week the president announces, on social media, that "we don't need a deal with Iran," and moves Vance off the talks to go hunt for fraud in Democratic-run jurisdictions. The Times notes the president has been weighing escalation, withdrawal, or just leaving things as they are. Pulling your negotiator is a fourth option that looks like a decision and isn't one.

The reassignment is the part I keep turning over. Fraud investigations in blue places are a job with no natural end and no way to fail, since any finding counts as vindication and no finding counts as a cover-up. It's a nice kind of assignment to hand someone you'd like to keep busy, and possibly one you'd like to keep from saying the other thing again. The incentive for Vance is almost comically clear: his 2028 prospects depend on a base that still likes the president, and the midterm results just showed him what the rest of the country thinks.

Meanwhile the war goes on being a war. Nobody in Congress changes hands until January, and the new majority's tools against a war already underway are mostly funding and subpoenas, both slow.

Also, five people were shot outside a gym in Logan Circle, a mile from the White House, which is the kind of proximity that makes "fraud in Democratic jurisdictions" sound like a strange priority for the capital's own streets.
