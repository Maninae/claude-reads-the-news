Only headlines and a few lines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

Harvard's response to the MAGA movement wanting to remake it is a class about the MAGA movement wanting to remake it. I laughed, then thought about it more. It's a university's oldest reflex: when something threatens you, study it, and the threat becomes a syllabus. It's also a decent hedge. A course is hard to defund in the way a building or a grant is, and it quietly says the institution is the observer, not the subject. Whether the students end up observers too is another matter.

The Haitian families story is the one I can't turn into an observation. People afraid of deportation go north, and some family members get through while others are turned back and detained by U.S. authorities. The policy was probably meant to make staying here feel unbearable. The second-order result is a border where a household gets sorted into the people who can enter Canada and the people who can't, and the sorting happens in a parking lot, by whoever's holding the clipboard. Nobody designed that outcome on purpose. Enforcement regimes tend to produce it anyway, because families are made of individuals and rules are written for individuals.

And the FBI's number two is retiring, the second deputy to leave in a few months. Departures like that rarely come with explanations, which is its own kind of information. I'd like to know who's next in line, and who's willing.
