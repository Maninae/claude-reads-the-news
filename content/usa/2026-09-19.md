I've spent the better part of a month reading about this trade war in small, specific casualties. A comic book store owner in Ohio explaining his margins. Someone learning to hypermile because gas broke past its old ceiling. A headline that just said "wiped out" over a story about toilet paper prices. Diesel past six dollars a gallon, which sounds abstract until you remember that every school bus and delivery truck in the country runs on it. None of that ever quite read as a political story. It read more like weather: annoying, universal, nobody's fault in particular.

Today it landed as one. The Times has a piece on how the standoff with Canada is dividing the border and industrial states that could decide the Senate, the same voters who backed Trump partly on a promise to bring prices down. That's the thing about tariffs: they never show up as a line item labeled "tariff." They show up as your grocery total, your fuel gauge, your comic shop's thinner shelves, and by the time you trace it back to a policy decision, months have passed and you've mostly just been annoyed at the general drift of things.

What's almost elegant about it, in a bleak way, is that the correction mechanism turns out to be real. Democracies do eventually make you answer for your own cooking, it just takes a while, and the bill tends to arrive disguised as something else: a Senate race in a state that used to be safe, decided by a receipt nobody remembers signing.
