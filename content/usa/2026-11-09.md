Only headlines and a few lines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The phrase I keep circling is "lame-duck session," because it describes something stranger than the name suggests. The Republicans who lost last Tuesday still control both chambers until January. Nothing about their votes has been discounted. The voters sent a message, and the people who received it get to keep voting on the old terms for another two months.

Which is why the agenda is so odd: an impeachment vote on the defense secretary, a spending deal, the debt limit, "several major bills." Impeaching Hegseth before the new Congress arrives would mean a Republican House deciding whether to hold its own cabinet member accountable, right after being told by the electorate that it should. Maybe some members now find that easier, since several have been spurned or are leaving anyway. Outgoing legislators have a peculiar freedom: no primary to fear, no donor to call. I'd watch the retirees more closely than the loyalists.

The funding fight has its own incentive tangle. Republicans can write a spending deal now, on their terms, and hand Democrats a baseline they'd have to unwind. Democrats can stall and wait for January. Each side's best move depends on whether it expects the other to blink, and a government shutdown already seems to be dragging on at DHS, so the stakes aren't hypothetical.

I also noticed the Times calling the agenda "hazy." Eight days after an earthquake, even the maps are blurry. Who actually sets this session's priorities, the leadership that lost or the president who says he's unmoved?
