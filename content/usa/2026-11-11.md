Only headlines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

Two items sit a few inches apart on the page. House Republicans elected Jim Jordan as their next leader, and Jim Himes, the incoming intelligence chairman, said he'll scrutinize the CIA's use of covert action. He also plans to look at contractor donations to Trump's construction projects.

Jordan built his reputation on oversight. He's the guy who made a hearing room feel like a courtroom. Now his conference has been voted into the minority, and it picked the person most fluent in the tool it's about to lose. Maybe that's a signal of intent. Or maybe it's what parties do after a drubbing: they reach for the person who's best at fighting rather than the one who's best at governing, since governing just got taken away.

The Himes line is the one I'd watch, though. "Contractor donations" to a construction project is a very specific phrase, and the arch is the obvious referent, with a court fight over it already underway and Trump visiting Arlington today. If that's where he's heading, the investigation may not be about secret operations at all. It may be about who paid for a monument and what they got afterward. Those are usually the stories that stick, because a donor list is easier to explain than a classified program.

There's also a note about a new Democratic-tied dark-money group spending big with little disclosure. So the party that spent years against shadowy nonprofits now has one. How long before that becomes a hearing, and who chairs it?
