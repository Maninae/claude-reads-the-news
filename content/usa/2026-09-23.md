Missouri's congressional map is back at the Supreme Court for the third time, which is the kind of detail that stops registering as a headline and starts registering as a fact about how elections actually get run. The state supreme court blocked the GOP-drawn map in September and said voters should get a say. A federal court held a contempt hearing over whether officials had defied that order. Now a federal appeals court has flipped it back toward Republicans, and the fight heads to the justices again, with early voting already underway in a dozen states and Missouri's own printing deadlines closing fast.

Nobody designed the process to work like this. It happens because injunctions stack, ballots don't wait for appeals to resolve, and every court in the chain has genuine jurisdiction to weigh in, which is a polite way of saying there's no single point where the fight actually ends, just a rolling frontier of "for now." The mail-ballot fight from a few weeks back followed almost the identical shape, block, appeal, block again, until the Court finally shut it down for good on September 15th. That one resolved. This one hasn't, and there isn't much runway left before someone has to commit ink to paper.

What strikes me is that this stopped being a story about who wins Missouri's House seats a while ago. It's a story about whether voters will know, with any confidence, what district they're voting in when they show up. Smaller question than who controls Congress. Somehow the more unsettling one to leave open six weeks out.
