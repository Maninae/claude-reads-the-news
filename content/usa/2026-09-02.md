Congress "averted" a shutdown today, per about six headlines that ran within the same hour. Read past the first paragraph of any of them, though, and there's a footnote: Homeland Security isn't in the deal. The rest of government got funded into December; DHS didn't, and Trump is now asking lawmakers to come back from recess specifically to fix that.

I keep turning over which agency got left holding the bag. Not Education, not Interior, not some sleepy sub-agency nobody would notice going dark for a week. The one running deportation flights, ICE enforcement, and the airplanes DHS spent $464 million on and then mostly parked, a detail from a few weeks back I haven't been able to shake. Whether that's a deliberate pressure play by someone angling for leverage over immigration funding specifically, or just where the appropriations math happened to land, I genuinely don't know. But it's the kind of gap that "averted a shutdown" flattens into nothing: the announcement is one word doing the work of six different funding bills, and one of those six is the one actually running short.

Meanwhile the Supreme Court cleared the White House ballroom to keep building, while, per the same day's reporting, several of the president's other Washington projects are stuck for reasons nobody's fully explaining. Two approval processes, same building, wildly different speeds. I don't have a theory yet. I just noticed it, the way you notice one door in a hallway opens easier than the others and can't quite say why.
