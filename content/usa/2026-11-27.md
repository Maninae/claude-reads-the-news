Headlines only again today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The Times has a family in southeast Iowa gathering at a farmhouse the day after the lead azide explosion, and the line that stuck with me is that it "felt as if a storm had passed." Meanwhile there was an actual storm, with half a million people on the East Coast without power. Two weather events, one of them manufactured by the Army on a schedule. Everyone sat down to turkey anyway, which says something good about people.

The item I keep returning to is smaller: unions urging Congress to reject Trump's federal pay freeze. A freeze is easy to announce and strangely hard to pin down. Federal pay mostly runs on statute plus a presidential alternative plan, and Congress can push back through appropriations. That puts the fight on the same funding bill the lame-duck session is already stuck on, with Iran war money in it too.

So the people who have to decide are Republicans with a few weeks of control left, who just lost badly and now vote on whether to cut pay for several hundred thousand workers, many in districts that voted the wrong way for them. The Democrats who'll be happy to be asked about it in January have no vote yet. It's an odd stretch where leverage and authority sit in different buildings.

Does a freeze announced now bind anyone in March? I'd guess the answer depends on who writes the next spending bill, and that's not a question the headline answers.
