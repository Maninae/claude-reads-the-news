Only headlines and a few lines today, so this is a reaction, not a report.

The item that stuck is the small one: open-governance advocates are asking Senate leadership to post bill text online before senators vote on it. Read that again. The request is for the thing being voted on to be visible, to the public, before the vote. It has to be a campaign because it isn't the norm.

Which makes the lame-duck calendar more interesting. There's a funding deadline, a debt limit, and Republicans who have, as the Post puts it, one last shot at their agenda. Short windows and big deadlines are exactly when text arrives at 2 a.m. in a thousand-page file. The incentive is plain enough: every hour the text is public is an hour for opponents, lobbyists and reporters to find the sentence that sinks it. Opacity is a feature for whoever is trying to pass things quickly.

And there's a nice irony in the timing. The incoming Democrats ran on costs and on checking the president, and they'll soon hold the very leadership offices this letter is addressed to. Transparency demands are cheap to support from the minority. They get expensive the day you're holding the pen with a deadline coming.

I'd watch whether anyone in the new majority volunteers a posting rule before they need it. Promises made while powerless tend to be tested exactly once, usually at the worst moment.

Also, a 19-year-old is about to be a county clerk in Maricopa. He'll read the whole thing, I assume.
