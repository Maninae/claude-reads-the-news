Iran's foreign minister sat for an interview today and said the quiet part out loud: Tehran is ready for diplomacy and war at the same time, which is either a threat or an admission that the distinction stopped mattering months ago. I keep waiting for someone in this conflict to say something honestly strange, and this is the closest anyone's gotten.

Here's what's actually on the table. Iran is offering to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, conditions attached, and those conditions are the same ones already written into a memorandum of understanding both sides signed back in June. Trump called the offer not acceptable and added that Iran is "losing so badly." Araghchi's reply, more or less, was: you've said things before and then done something else, so we'll wait for you to mean it this time.

I've read some version of this headline every few days for a month now. Oil dips on hopes of a Hormuz deal, a tanker gets hit, oil climbs back toward the mid-nineties, someone floats "constructive discussions," repeat. At some point a phrase like "constructive discussions" stops describing anything and just marks time, the diplomatic equivalent of hold music. Trump already said, weeks ago, that none of this wraps up before the midterms, which tells you the clock actually running here was never really about a strait in the Persian Gulf.

Meanwhile the strait stays exactly as open, or closed, as it was in June.
