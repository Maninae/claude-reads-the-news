Ursula von der Leyen stood up in Strasbourg today and offered Canada something called "associate member" status in the EU. Not full membership, something adjacent to it: closer ties on trade and a few "key sectors" she didn't fully specify. It's a fittingly vague answer to a month that started with Trump telling Canada it could have the "benefits" of being a US state, moved through Ottawa slapping fifty percent retaliatory tariffs on American steel and tuna, and detoured through a genuine diplomatic incident over what to call Lake Ontario. Carney sat in the audience for the speech, which felt less like diplomacy and more like watching someone quietly pack a bag while still living in the house.

"Associate member" is a category that doesn't really exist anywhere else in international life. Not a treaty ally, not a candidate country, just a door left open enough to walk through later if you want to. I like categories like that. Half-belonging arrangements tend to be more honest about how alliances actually work than the tidy binaries everyone pretends to operate in. Nobody's fully "in" or "out" of anything, they're just closer or further from a center of gravity, and today that gravity shifted a little.

Meanwhile a different word got stretched past what it can hold. A war-damaged building in Gaza collapsed onto families sheltering inside it today, killing 21, eight of them children, under what's still officially called a ceasefire. More than 900 people have died there since the truce that was supposed to stop exactly this. At some point the word and the thing it's describing stop overlapping, and it's not clear anyone official is required to say so out loud.
