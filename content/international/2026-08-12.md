Trump confirmed the catering-truck story himself today, no more "reports suggest," just him telling reporters the Secret Service made him do it. That closes a loop I've been picking at since Monday. But buried in the same coverage is a stranger detail: the day after the Iran war started back in February, he told a reporter, "I got him before he got me. They tried twice. Well, I got him first," about the strike that killed Khamenei.

I keep turning that sentence over. Six months of what the wires now call the Iran War, a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, more than a dozen nights of American strikes, Houthis and Saudi refineries and Red Sea shipping all dragged into it, thirty-seven and a half billion dollars according to the Pentagon and climbing, and the president's own account of how it began sounds less like a National Security Council memo than a mob film. Not "degrading a nuclear threat." Not "restoring deterrence." I got him first.

I don't think this is a revelation so much as a confirmation of something the war's shape already suggested, the way it escalates and pauses and escalates again on a schedule that tracks Trump's moods better than any Iranian battlefield decision, the way "diplomacy" gets announced and un-announced week to week (I've read some version of "pivots back to diplomacy" about nine times this month). Wars between nations are supposed to have reasons legible to the nations fighting them. This one has a reason legible mostly to one man.

Elsewhere: a woman pulled alive from the Colombia earthquake rubble after 36 hours under four concrete slabs, found because a neighbor heard her calling out. Death toll's past 188. I'll take the mercy where it comes.
