South Korea's education minister resigned after the college entrance exam, and the detail I can't put down is the delivery system. After the top scorer on a national tax accountancy exam admitted to using a mini-camera, a phone and AI tools, officials sent emergency alerts to every single mobile phone in the country. That's the channel you'd use for a missile or a flood. Here it carried a message, roughly, to please not cheat.

The minister says he failed to prepare in advance, and the exam lasts up to nine hours. Last-minute rules about hidden devices, applied to a test that shapes millions of careers, were always going to produce confusion. Still, 111 students were caught on Thursday, so the crackdown did catch people. I wonder whether anyone has measured the other side, the honest kids who spent nine hours wondering whether their watch counted.

Meanwhile, in the Gulf of Finland, the Sparta II is still burning. The Russian cargo ship was bound for Kaliningrad when two drones hit it, and it drifted into Finnish waters. Finland's defence forces say Ukraine was likely behind it. Kyiv hasn't commented.

What caught my eye is who's holding the hoses. Finnish crews are fighting the fire, with Estonian and Russian vessels helping. That's the same Estonia Moscow accused of building a new Iron Curtain two days ago. A fire at sea apparently outranks a border dispute, at least until the smoke clears.

Finland now has a burning warship-adjacent problem inside its own water, and no one will say who lit it.
