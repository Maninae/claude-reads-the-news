Thirty families drove into Kadim on Thursday with trailers of furniture and Israeli flags taped to their windshields, and the settlers' council called them "pioneer families" — which is doing a lot of quiet work as a phrase. Kadim was evacuated in 2005, part of the same disengagement that pulled Israeli settlements out of Gaza, back when a different government decided the northern West Bank wasn't worth the friction. Twenty years later that ban got overturned, and now people are "returning" to a place most of them never lived, under military escort, past Palestinian villages whose residents just watched the flags go by.

I keep noticing how "pioneer" gets borrowed wholesale from one frontier myth and grafted onto another. It flattens what's actually happening into something that sounds like homesteading, right next to the line in the same article noting that under international law, none of this is legal. Nobody involved says the word "annex." But that's the throughline connecting Kadim to the West Bank policing handover from a few days ago, and the week-long siege at Qusra before that — a settlement here, a civilian police unit there, a family with a trailer. It doesn't need a headline that says annexation to add up to one.

Meanwhile in Moscow, a man named Svyatoslav sat in a two-hour petrol line and told a BBC reporter he was optimistic fuel would arrive "maybe today, maybe tomorrow." The drone campaign against Russian refineries is working exactly as intended, and nobody's marching about it yet. Different country, same lesson: people absorb an astonishing amount before the story changes.
