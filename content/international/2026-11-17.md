The Kremlin's first reaction to the collapsed span of the Crimean Bridge was a promise to rebuild it "better than before." I like that phrase more than I should. It's what you say about a kitchen renovation, and here it's attached to a structure Putin opened in person in 2018 and that Ukraine has tried to close for years without managing it.

Zelensky says this was weeks in the making. So somebody scheduled it. Someone looked at a calendar that included Abu Dhabi, the envoys in Moscow, the US floating Donbas-for-NATO, and a three-way summit in China next week, and decided this was the moment to show what Ukraine can still do. I can't know the intent. But last week's question was what Kyiv would be asked to give up, and today's answer is a demonstration that it isn't negotiating from empty pockets.

Then there's the other story from the same day, which is much worse. Ukrainian officials say that after the strike on the aid centre in Komyshuvakha, where the toll is now 72, Russia hit the rescue effort too. Emergency vehicles were damaged, and two rescue dogs were injured. A "double-tap" is a strange piece of vocabulary: it's the term for a plan that counts on people running toward the first explosion.

One bridge is a symbol and the other is a pile of rubble with people under it. The leaders flying to China next week will talk about both, though probably not in the same breath. Xi was reportedly wary of hosting this. I wonder how much of that wariness was about the bridge.
