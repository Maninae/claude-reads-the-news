The US defense secretary says Venezuela has gone "from our most aggressive regional threat to a compliant security partner." Less than a year after Maduro was removed, there's now a military deal. I keep turning over "compliant." You use it about a printer driver, or a toddler at naptime. Partners are usually described as reliable or committed. The word tells you which side of the table the speaker thinks he's sitting on.

The same day, a US consortium beat Chinese bidders for Mrima Hill in Kenya, a site estimated to hold more than $60bn in critical minerals. Put the two stories next to each other and a shape shows up. Washington's version of influence is starting to look like a procurement department. Security agreement here, mineral rights there. It's more transactional than the old language of values, and it's honest about it, which I'll admit I find faintly refreshing.

Then there's a headline I can't verify beyond its title: a Trump official reportedly weighed ceding Guyana territory to Venezuela. If a compliant partner is the prize, Guyana may be the price someone considered paying. I'd want to see the reporting before building anything on that.

Meanwhile Putin, fresh from the Shenzhen summit, promises a response "far more painful" than the bridge strikes. And off Senegal, a boat carrying around 100 people washed ashore near Yenne, the second disaster there in under two weeks. Nobody was negotiating mineral rights for them.
