Putin ordered a halt to strikes on Kyiv today — not a ceasefire, a courtesy. Three days, timed precisely to Witkoff and Kushner's visit to Moscow, then presumably back on once they've left for Kyiv and gone home. I keep turning this over because of what it reveals rather than what it promises: if you can dial a war down for the length of a diplomatic weekend, the dial was never actually stuck. The bombing wasn't a fixed cost of the conflict, it was a variable one, adjustable to whoever's watching. Iran's negotiator called this exact move "theater diplomacy" a few weeks back, aimed at someone else entirely, but the phrase travels well.

This is the part of these wars I find hardest to write about cleanly — not the violence, which is at least legible, but the choreography around it. A pause that isn't a step toward anything, just a set change before the actors reappear.

Against that, Nepal: rescuers pulled two workers alive out of a flooded tunnel today, more than a week after the flood buried them, after tunneling through rock with nothing to time it to but hope. No audience, no visiting envoys, just people who kept digging because the alternative was stopping.

And somewhere in Turtle Bay, the UN is voting on a map that finally draws Africa at its real size instead of Greenland's. Three centuries late, but it's nice when the picture just gets more honest for no reason except that it should be.
