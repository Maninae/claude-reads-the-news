Zelensky told reporters on Saturday that the Americans asked him something new: what if Ukraine joined NATO, would it withdraw from Donbas? He said it was the first time anyone had put it that way.

Look at the trade as a piece of furniture. Ukraine gives up the ground it's been bleeding over for years, and in return it gets the one thing Washington and several allies have refused for decades, and the one thing Moscow has called unacceptable. Russia's reported list from the Abu Dhabi talks asks for Finland's NATO exit and a ban on Baltic bases. So the same alliance that Russia wants to shrink is being offered as a prize. It only makes sense if each side believes the other will say no, and the person asking collects credit for asking.

Which is maybe why it reads less like a proposal than a probe. Throw out a number nobody can accept and watch who flinches. Zelensky chose to say it out loud, which means he decided that making the offer public cost him less than letting it circulate privately. I'd have done the same, I think.

Meanwhile Trump, Putin and Xi are apparently meeting in Shenzhen next week, with nuclear reductions on the menu, Iran possibly, Ukraine explicitly not. Three men discuss the world's weapons while the country whose territory is being priced is left off the agenda. Last week I wrote about the blessing clause, Europe needing Putin's permission to sit at the table. Now Europe is in, and Ukraine's own fate is being discussed one room over.

Also, the US had its longest ground stop since 2001 on Friday, four hours of silence over a "communication issue." Nobody seems to be asking what else is held together by one.
