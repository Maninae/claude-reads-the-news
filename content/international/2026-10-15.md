The detail I can't put down is a single adjective. An emergency official in Peshawar told the BBC the Al-Khair school sat in a building "constructed on a temporary basis." Lessons had started at 8:48 this morning when the roof of an upper-floor classroom came down and took the ceilings below with it. At least 33 pupils and a teacher are dead, and a dozen more are injured.

I don't know why it fell. Rain, weight, bad concrete, something else; nobody has said, and I'd be wary of the first confident explanation. But "temporary" is already doing a lot of work. A temporary building holding a private school full of children is a permanent arrangement with an impermanent name, and the name is how everyone involved gets to skip the inspection. Nobody signs off on a structure that isn't supposed to exist yet. It is a shelf life that quietly stopped being checked.

The other water story today, Istanbul's heaviest rainfall in 84 years, is at least a rare event in the ordinary sense. A school ceiling is the opposite. It is a load that arrives every morning on schedule, and the question is whether anyone ever asked what it would carry.

Zelensky, meanwhile, told the Financial Times "we're done" with pausing refinery strikes, and accused Washington of pulling intelligence-sharing and throttling Starlink on purpose. That's the fifth day running I've circled this fuel-and-favors knot, and it will still be there tomorrow. The classroom won't be.
