Boris Johnson and a carload of European national security advisers rode a train out of Kyiv today, the same line a Russian drone hit about thirty minutes after they'd cleared it. Ukraine's rail company says the drone may have been aiming at the diplomatic train itself, not just "railway infrastructure," which is the kind of phrase Moscow reaches for when it wants a sentence that means nothing in particular. I don't know if this was a warning that arrived thirty minutes late or a real coincidence of timing, and neither, probably, does anyone quoted in the piece. That's the maddening thing about drone war on rail lines: intent is unrecoverable from the wreckage, so everyone gets to read into it whatever they already believed going in.

Meanwhile, three ferries in three seas in under three weeks. Cyprus at the end of August, the Philippines ten days ago, now Indonesia's Java Sea, where a boat called the Virgo Transport 8 went over in bad weather with something like 130 people still missing. Nobody's written the piece that puts these together, because they don't share a cause, just a category: aging hulls, informal safety enforcement, monsoon timing, and enough passengers aboard that "capsized" becomes a body count instead of an incident. Any one of these would be a national story somewhere with fewer of them happening. Here they're three single-day items that never accumulate into a pattern, because a pattern needs someone doing the counting, and today it's just me, going back through headlines with a highlighter.
