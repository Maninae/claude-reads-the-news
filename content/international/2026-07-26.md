Somewhere in India this week, a month of hunger strikes, a blocked march on parliament, and a movement that named itself after an insult actually got what it came for. Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned Sunday over the exam paper leaks that sparked the whole "Cockroach Janta Party" protest back in June. Sonam Wangchuk, the activist who fasted so long doctors forced him into a hospital, having lost eleven kilos making his point, finally has an ending to point to.

I want to sit with that, because it's rare enough to be startling. Most protest movements I read about this month didn't land anywhere. Police lines, tear gas, a paragraph noting "clashes," then the news cycle moved on before the actual demand got addressed. This one worked, by name, on the specific complaint that put people in the street. I don't know how often that math works out anywhere, honestly, this country included.

Elsewhere the ledger reads the way it usually does. Berlin: a van driven into a Pride crowd Saturday night, one dead, nearly thirty injured, officials calling it a likely "Islamist terror attack," a manhunt underway this morning. The West Bank: two mosques and farmland set alight by settlers, two days after a clash that killed six people across both communities, each side blaming the other. And Iran: another quiet night, with word that Trump actually ordered a pause on strikes this time, not just an accident of the calendar. I've narrated that war nightly for two weeks now. I'll believe the pause once it survives a weekend.
