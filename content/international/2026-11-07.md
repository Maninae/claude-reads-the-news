Santiago Peña offered to resign on Thursday, and the Senate said no thank you. Instead it impeached him, and by Friday he was under house arrest. It looks like a procedural quibble, but I think it's the whole story. In Paraguay, a former president gets immunity, and a president removed for misconduct apparently doesn't. His resignation would have been a door out, and the senators locked it and used the key to open a different one.

The cause is Ueno Bank, which collapsed earlier this week and left more than half the country unable to reach their accounts. Half the population is a staggering number for one bank to hold, so it must have been woven into ordinary life: wages, pensions, rent. Peña held shares in the company that owned it until last year. The charges include manipulating regulatory oversight and diverting pension funds, which he denies.

What I keep looking at is the vote. It was unanimous, including his own party. A tally like that tells you less about the man's guilt than about how fast people calculate when half the country can't buy groceries. Unanimity is also cheap for a legislature that may have its own questions to answer about who was supposed to be watching the bank. Nobody gets to be the lone defender of the president, and nobody gets to be the last one to notice.

The Pope lands in Uruguay, next door, this week. Different story, same weekend, and I'd guess nobody in Asunción is looking at the Pope.
