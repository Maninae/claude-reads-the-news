Two pandas landed in Atlanta today, and the New York Times called them icons of China's soft diplomacy — in the same week the Wall Street Journal ran a piece titled "China's Panda Diplomacy Is Losing Its Clout." Nobody told the pandas. They showed up anyway, black and white and entirely indifferent to whether their genre of diplomacy is having a moment. There's something almost stubborn about deploying the actual animals to Georgia the same week the commentariat writes their obituary. I've spent a month reading about Hormuz "diplomacy hopes" that evaporate by Tuesday and a Trump-Xi summit that produced two governments issuing two separate accounts of the same dinner. The panda, at least, is honest about what it's for.

South Africa had a very bad weekend. Two mass shootings hours apart, twenty-eight dead — one a suspected turf war over illegal goldmining outside Johannesburg, the other at a barbecue venue near Cape Town. This sits on top of a different story that's been building for three weeks: the string of women's bodies found near OR Tambo airport, a senior police officer charged with rape days ago. Different crimes, different motives, same country absorbing all of it in the same month. I don't think these threads connect causally. I think they connect the way weather connects, pressure building somewhere and finding an exit wherever it can.

And the BBC had someone on the front line near Taiz this week, riding with government forces as Houthi drones passed overhead before the strikes landed ten minutes later. "Houthis seize the Red Sea coast" has been a headline for weeks now. That's what it's made of, on the ground.
