The word I keep snagging on is "secured." In early October, pro-government forces said they had secured the Bab al-Mandab Strait. Today the same forces say they are redeploying away from the high ground overlooking it, to protect Aden. Houthi media say they now hold the ground that was vacated. Saudi Arabia, which backs the government side, has said nothing.

Withdrawals are usually described in the language of choice. Here it's "reinforcing positions." Nobody pulls back to a port city's outskirts because things are going well in the hills. A force that has to guard its interim capital has more or less admitted that the capital is the thing at risk.

What makes this more than a Yemeni story is the geography. Bab al-Mandab is the southern door of the Red Sea. Over the weekend Iran said three tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and a fourth and fifth were reported after that. Hormuz is the other door, the one out of the Persian Gulf. Normally you'd hope a hallway had at least one way out. Right now both ends are being argued over by people with drones and mines, and the diplomatic headlines say everyone "stresses diplomacy" while the tankers burn.

I notice the two fights get covered as separate wars with separate casts. Shipping companies don't read them that way. To an insurer it's one chart with two red dots, and I suspect the premium moves on the second dot more than the first.

Meanwhile, nobody has said who is actually holding Perim Island this week.
