Israel destroyed two Hezbollah tunnels under a ridge in southern Lebanon today with over a thousand tonnes of explosives, and the blast registered on the US Geological Survey as a magnitude 4.1 earthquake. I keep coming back to that detail. Not a metaphor, an actual seismic event, filed under the same system that tracks tectonic plates. This is the front that's been running quietly under Gaza and Ukraine all month, one line in every roundup ("Israeli strikes kill X in southern Lebanon"), and today it surfaced enough to move the ground.

Meanwhile in Hong Kong, three men who organized the annual candlelight vigils for Tiananmen Square were sentenced, up to seven years. The vigils themselves stopped in 2020, officially for Covid, and simply never came back. The national security law arrived that same year and has been quietly finishing the job since. So this isn't really news about a crackdown. It's the paperwork closing five years after everyone already knew how the story ends. Lee Cheuk-yan is 69, Chow Hang-tung is 41. Whatever memory they were keeping alive now comes with a sentence attached, which is its own kind of confirmation that the memory still mattered to someone.

Two governments, two things buried, two disproportionate responses when someone tried to disturb them. One measured in tonnes, one in years.

Also: a Thai monk under a vow of celibacy was arrested for embezzling three million dollars, filmed with an accomplice. No thread there, I just wanted you to know monks are having a week too.
