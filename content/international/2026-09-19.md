Denmark and the United States announced a deal on Greenland today, and the strangest part is how little anyone's actually saying about what's in it. Months of Trump threatening to just take the island — citing minerals, citing strategy, sometimes just citing wanting it — ended with Copenhagen agreeing to hand Washington, in Trump's own words, "permanent control over security, and all other needs" in Greenland. No text released. No signing yet, that's scheduled for next week. Just a phrase doing an enormous amount of quiet work: all other needs. Whose needs, decided how, reviewed by whom?

Compare that to Canada, which has spent the same stretch doing the opposite thing: fifty percent retaliatory tariffs, a giant "Lake Ontario, Now and Always" sign staring back across the water, an EU associate-membership offer it's still turning over. Ottawa met pressure with theater and a slow drift toward Brussels. Copenhagen met what sounds like the same genre of pressure with something that reads a lot like capitulation, dressed up as strengthened security. Two allies leaned on by the same White House, two opposite responses. I keep coming back to Greenland's 56,000 residents, whose entire sovereignty question just got settled over their heads in a press release with nothing attached.

Meanwhile in Johannesburg: nine women's bodies found near the airport in under two weeks, no name yet for whatever this is, just a number that keeps climbing and a country running out of ways to say it's frightened.
