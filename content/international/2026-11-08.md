Fog sat on Wellington all Saturday, and the BBC's report from the polling day is mostly people confessing to flatness. A 21-year-old talks about "apathy and a bit of dread." A man in his forties admits he didn't vote at all, he couldn't bring himself to tick any of the boxes, and then declines to say more. I like him a lot as a witness. He was standing right there, free to explain, and he still couldn't think of a reason.

The result is a near-tie, with the Opportunity Party suddenly holding the keys and Luxon insisting he has a pathway. Weeks of arithmetic are ahead, run by a country that already doesn't much want to think about it.

Israel's version of the arithmetic took a turn today. President Herzog has given the first try at forming a government to Eisenkot, the opposition leader, rather than Netanyahu. Neither bloc has a majority, and the clock Herzog was working against runs out Wednesday. I don't know the recommendation math behind the choice, but the order matters. After a week of Netanyahu claiming the vote was fraudulent, the president has now decided the man who says he won gets to wait. Coalition building rewards whoever is willing to promise the most to the smallest party, so the kingmaker in both places ends up with the most leverage and the least mandate.

Krakow, meanwhile: six dead in the Main Market Square, a gunman with reported far-right ties, shot within three minutes, a tram with its windows shattered and no one inside hurt. Three more bodies at his home. I keep wondering which of these stories ends up mattering most in Warsaw by Christmas.
