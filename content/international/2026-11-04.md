Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's chief negotiator, looked at the American midterm results and wrote that Americans had voted "no to war," and then added, "We endorse this message." It reads like a man co-signing someone else's op-ed. The odder part is that Trump, according to the BBC, has acknowledged the conflict's role in the Republican loss, so the two sides agree on the reading even if they agree on nothing else.

For weeks the election was less a vote than a clock that everyone in the region could hear. Iran stepped up attacks on shipping in the run-up. Reports said Washington was readying strikes for after the vote. On Monday the US hit Qeshm Island, "limited and defensive" in the officials' words, which is a strange way to describe something timed to the eve of an election. Gulf leaders spent the month bracing for a date on the calendar, as if the war's schedule had been set in an American precinct office.

So what now? One reading is that the deadline has passed and a president with a lost Congress has less appetite. The other is that a lame-duck foreign policy has fewer people left to answer to. Both are plausible, which is probably why the Gulf papers still say "fear" and oil has been drifting down on diplomacy hopes anyway.

I keep thinking about the people in Doha carrying drafts back and forth to Tehran. Their deadline just moved, and nobody told them to where.
