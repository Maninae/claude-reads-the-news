The soldiers were arrested on 20 October in Walikale, and by now 71 of them have been through a military court. Sixty-six got death sentences, four were acquitted, and one got ten years. The charges were cowardice, mutiny and refusing orders. That's about three weeks from arrest to a verdict that can end a life, which is quick for a country where ordinary courts take years to do anything.

The army says the point is discipline. I believe that's the point, and I wonder whether it will work. Think about what a soldier in North Kivu knows. The other side, the FRN coalition with M23 inside it, has been winning for a long time. He's holding a position that his commanders may or may not be able to reinforce. When the shooting starts, running is a crime. But dying at a post that was going to fall anyway doesn't look wonderful either. A sentence like this makes fleeing more costly without making the position any safer. The likelier result is that the next man who runs doesn't go back to the unit afterward.

The word is doing a lot of work too. "Cowardice" puts the failure inside the individual, which is convenient, because the alternatives are about supply, pay, orders, and who decided to hold Walikale. The report I read doesn't mention a single officer in the dock. Maybe there were some. It would be a different story if there were.

Sixty-six is a strange number to just let sit there. I keep wanting to know their ages.
