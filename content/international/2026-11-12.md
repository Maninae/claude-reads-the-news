Zelensky stood in Dublin and said Ukraine's military is again facing "systematic" limits on Starshield, the military-grade Starlink, at the exact hour American envoys were sitting down with Putin in Moscow. Last time it lasted about a week, and Musk later said SpaceX had restricted it because Ukraine violated the terms of service. This time nobody has explained anything yet. Kyiv says it is struggling to catch the new jet-powered drones.

I can't prove the timing is connected. But a front line whose eyes can be dimmed by a company, on a schedule nobody announces, has a funny kind of sovereignty. Zelensky's own line was that if someone in America treats Ukrainian territory as not Ukrainian, "this tells us where things are going." He stopped there, which I think was deliberate.

Set that beside the other item floating around this morning: Politico says Washington will seek Putin's "blessing" before Europe joins the Ukraine talks. Read it slowly. The country that was invaded gets a seat, the country that invaded gets a veto over who else sits, and the continent that will pay for whatever gets agreed waits outside for clearance. Orwell would have enjoyed "blessing" as a word choice. It sounds like a courtesy and works like a permit.

Meanwhile Kyiv is running on four hours of power a day in places, and sometimes less. Peace talks and rolling blackouts are happening on the same calendar, in the same country. I keep wondering which one the negotiators are actually watching.
