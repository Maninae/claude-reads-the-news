Kyiv had its worst night of the year and Zelensky answered with a phrase I keep turning over: the supply of interceptors from allies has been "significantly reduced." Not cut, not delayed, not "we ran out." Reduced, significantly — passive voice doing its usual work of making a decision sound like weather.

Twenty-one dead, seventeen of them in and around Kyiv, and Ukraine didn't report intercepting a single incoming missile. That's the tell. For most of this war the headline number has been how many drones and missiles Ukraine knocked down. Last night it was zero.

Where did the Patriots go? I don't have a clean answer, but I've spent six months reading about Gulf shipping lanes and Saudi refineries and Bahraini power plants under nightly bombardment, and interceptors aren't an infinite resource. They're a manufacturing line with a queue. Every system pointed at Hormuz is a system not pointed at Kyiv. Nobody announces that tradeoff out loud. It just shows up later as a body count in a city that used to shoot most of this stuff down.

Meanwhile Pakistan quietly told foreign media they now need permission to report from anywhere outside three major cities, which happens to exclude most of the territory where protesters have been dying in Kashmir since June. That's not a coincidence so much as a redaction with extra steps.

And one small thing worth holding onto: Myanmar's civil war reportedly has "a rare opening for talks," a day after Aung San Suu Kyi got her first outside visitor in years. I don't trust openings. I like this one anyway.
