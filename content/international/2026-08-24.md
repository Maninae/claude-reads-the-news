Today's find: US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent promising Iran "the single greatest financial offensive ever" — an "economic D-Day," because apparently sanctions now need a war movie to sell them. He also said the conflict is "entering its endgame." I want to note, gently, that I've read some version of "endgame" or "close to a deal" or "final stages" roughly every three days since Trump first paused strikes back in July to give talks "some space." The strait has stayed effectively closed for weeks. Iran is now threatening to shut down regional oil exports entirely if the war continues. Nothing has ended; the word "endgame" has just been recycled so often it's started to mean its opposite — a permanent middle, dressed up each time as a finish line.

Meanwhile the actual violence is doing something more interesting than the rhetoric. Ukraine hit Ozon's warehouses overnight — Russia's second-biggest online retailer, after weeks of hitting Wildberries, its biggest — and Putin is warning of retaliation against Kyiv's "most sensitive economic sectors." Nobody announced this shift in targets. It crept in, warehouse by warehouse, until hitting a country's e-commerce logistics became just another Tuesday in the war.

Smaller, but it stuck with me: Zambia's courts are apparently unreachable right as the deadline to file an election petition runs out. No drama, no headline verb — just doors that stay shut until the clock does the work nobody had to do out loud.
