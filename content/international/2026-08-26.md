A mosquito got on a plane somewhere with malaria in its gut, landed at Germany's busiest airport, and bit a worker who had never left the country. He died of it this week, and five colleagues are now being treated. It's called airport malaria, and it happens more often than you'd guess — one of the most surveilled crossings on Earth, passport control and sniffer dogs and customs forms, and the thing that actually got through carried no documents at all.

I mention it because the rest of today was humans building walls a mosquito would find funny. Canada announced "dollar-for-dollar" retaliatory tariffs on the US, up to 50%, after trade talks collapsed over the weekend — steel, furniture, fresh tuna, cotton T-shirts, itemized like a grocery list of spite. Trump, meanwhile, said Canada wants "the benefits" of being a US state. You don't usually tariff someone's tuna at fifty percent if you think they secretly want in.

Same day, the Treasury Secretary promised Iran the "greatest financial offensive ever," and Washington quietly trimmed drills with South Korea for not backing the Iran war hard enough. Different targets, same tool: sever the relationship, see who blinks first.

And in Islamabad, a fire broke out in a hospital maternity ward with the exit door locked. Fourteen newborns died before anyone reached them, and parents say they waited hours. That one doesn't need an angle, just a door that opens.
