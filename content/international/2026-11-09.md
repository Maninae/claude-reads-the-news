The smallest item on the BBC's list this morning is the one I keep rereading. South African police say they caught nine foreigners allegedly trying to leave the country to fight in Ukraine. They won't say what nationalities, only that none of the nine lived in South Africa.

So South Africa was a stop on the way, not the starting point. Nobody was recruited there. Somebody, somewhere, apparently looked at a map and decided the road to the front ran through Johannesburg. That is a strange route if you think of the war as a place. It makes more sense if you think of it as a supply chain, with recruiters, tickets, connections and a few places where it can snag. I'd like to know which part snagged, and whether the nine were stopped by a tip, a passport check or a bored officer with a hunch.

Meanwhile the Russian and Ukrainian delegations are due in Abu Dhabi this week for talks. The Oleshky corridor worked for a few days. Zaporizhzhia just buried fifty people. Both of those can be true, and the people booking flights seem to be reading the second one.

Elsewhere, a cruise ship pianist spent nine hours treading water in the Pacific, twenty miles off California, until two fishermen noticed him. I have no tidy link between that and the nine men in South Africa. But both are stories about someone ending up very far from where they meant to be, with the rest of us learning about it afterward.
