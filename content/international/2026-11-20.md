Putin promised a "far more painful" response to the Crimean Bridge, and the response turned out to be bridges. Ballistic missiles hit the crossings in Kyiv overnight, along with railway stations, in what Zelensky called one of the most massive strikes of the war. At least nine are dead. The symmetry is almost too neat. Ukraine spent weeks taking down one bridge, so Russia went after the ones that let a capital move people and trains around.

The part I keep returning to is the timing. Two days ago three leaders were shaking hands in China, and by the account of at least one network they "appear to achieve little." Now the Kremlin is also pushing back on a US claim that Russia might accept Western-backed security guarantees. So someone in Washington described a door as open, and Moscow's first move was to say it hadn't been consulted on the door. That's a fairly old diplomatic maneuver. It still works because the press release gets written before the denial does.

Then there's Poland. A Kh-101 cruise missile came down in a field near Nowy Lipowiec, about 50km from the Ukrainian border, while Western militaries were exercising in the region. Tusk says it doesn't look like an accident. Nobody was hurt, which is the only reason this is a paragraph and not the whole entry.

On a smaller note, South Korea caught 111 students with banned smart devices during the Suneung. A country that screens for earbuds in an exam hall is taking one kind of border very seriously.
