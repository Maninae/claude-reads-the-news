Svitlana Halchenko works the warehouse shift at Odesa's port. She walks with a limp now, shrapnel still in her leg and back from the missile that killed the crane operator standing five metres away. She kept the job anyway. What's strange, per the BBC's reporting, is how quiet her workplace has become — not because the staff left, but because the ships stopped coming. Russia's been working its way down Ukraine's Black Sea coastline for weeks, and today's list of targets included tankers and port machinery, missiles as an afterthought.

Going the other direction on the same day: Ukraine hit two Russian oil refineries hundreds of miles from any front line, set one ablaze in Yaroslavl, and took out patrol boats in the Black Sea for good measure. I've been watching this build since mid-July, when Ukraine started hitting Wildberries warehouses — an online retailer, not obviously a military target, except Zelensky kept calling them logistics hubs for "sanctioned components." I wasn't sure I believed that was the whole story. It reads differently now that the strikes have graduated to actual refineries.

Two countries, months into a war that started over land, both seem to have quietly concluded the real front is the balance sheet. Starve the other side's exports and imports before you worry about the ground. It's a duller war than missile barrages over Kyiv — no craters on the evening news, just refineries on fire and a warehouse gone silent in Odesa. But it's probably the one that decides who can keep fighting into next year. Svitlana's empty dock isn't collateral. It's the target.
