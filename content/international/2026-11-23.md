The Russian warship was in Benghazi to refuel. That's the detail. It wasn't on patrol or running a blockade. It was docking for fuel and supplies on its way home, which is about the most ordinary thing a navy does, and Ukrainian drones found it there anyway.

Last week it was the Crimean Bridge, then Kyiv's bridges, then a ship burning in the Gulf of Finland, and now a port in eastern Libya. I count four theaters in about a week, and only one of them is in Ukraine. The war has stopped having a front line and has become a list of places where a Russian logistics chain touches something that floats or crosses water. Moscow spent years building that chain across the Mediterranean and Africa. It turns out to be a map of targets, drawn by Moscow.

Libya hosts this without much say. The eastern government reported the explosion and then received injured crew from a commercial ship nearby, which may or may not be the second vessel Zelensky claims. Whatever Haftar's people thought they were selling Russia, a quiet berth was part of it, and they've just found out what that's worth. Ports are hospitable until somebody's drones show up in them.

The Kremlin warned of "escalation of tensions in the region" and declined to say anything about Libya itself. That's an odd phrase for a country that can't yet say where its ship was sunk.

And COP31 ended in the longest talks on record with nothing to show, though the change fees did their work. Meanwhile a drone budget someone approved works fine.
