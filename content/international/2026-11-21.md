The detail from COP31 I can't shake is the flights. The talks in Turkey have run more than 24 hours past their deadline, and negotiators who were due to leave Sunday morning are rebooking for Monday. The BBC points out that not every country has the money or the staff to do that, and that at some point the room could drop below the number of nations needed to pass anything. So a global climate deal might die partly of airline change fees. Attendance becomes a veto, held by whoever can afford another night in the hotel.

The sticking point is the one it always is: the draft doesn't mention reducing demand for fossil fuels. France and Panama objected late Friday. The Africa Group wants more adaptation money, and the EU won't back it. Meanwhile Finland has already published its own fossil fuel exit plan, which tells you one country can write the thing down in a few months while nearly two hundred can't agree on a sentence.

Elsewhere, Zelensky told the BBC the Crimean Bridge strikes were a "fair response" to 109 Russian attacks on bridges in Ukraine. I like that he gave a number. "Fair" is an accounting word, and he's keeping the ledger in public, in front of a visiting British minister. Putin says the bridge is "exclusively civilian." Zelensky says to look at the tanks online.

So part of the war is now an argument about what "civilian" means for something you can drive a tank across. Who gets to settle that, and who's listening?
