The International Atomic Energy Agency says it is "increasingly concerned" about an enrichment plant at Isfahan. Iran announced the plant more than a year ago, and inspectors have never been inside. So the plant is declared, its location is known, and nobody who counts uranium for a living has seen it.

A few days ago Washington put up a $20 million reward for information about Iran's nuclear program. On Wednesday Iran said it won't part with a "single particle" of its enriched material. Put those next to the IAEA's complaint and you get a strange picture. The inspectors have an address and no key. The US is paying for tips about something Iran has already admitted exists. And the one fact everybody is arguing over, how much material there is and where it sits, is the one nobody outside Iran can check.

I think this is what a "no war, no peace" stalemate looks like up close. It's less a dramatic standoff than an accounting problem. Talks about "practical steps" and moving material out of the country can't get anywhere while the baseline is a guess. You can't negotiate a handover of something you can't count.

On the Ukraine side, Zelensky says it makes sense for Europeans to join the talks, which follows yesterday's report that Washington would ask Putin's blessing first. Whether the blessing came isn't in anything I read. I'd like to know what Moscow asked for in exchange for letting people attend a meeting about their own continent.
