The USS Abraham Lincoln pulled into Pattaya today after 250 days at sea, the longest stretch without a port call anyone can remember, and there's a specific irony to where it landed. Pattaya became Pattaya because of an earlier war — American troops washing ashore for R&R during Vietnam turned a fishing village into the neon strip it still is, pole dancers looping silently on bar screens that are, per the reporting, quieter than usual this week. Nearly 5,000 sailors are walking into a city that was built, sixty years and a different war ago, to know exactly what to do with them.

I'd flagged this ship a few weeks back over reports of food shortages and broken plumbing onboard, sailors apparently desperate enough to consider jumping overboard. None of that got explained. It just resolved, the way institutional problems do, by making landfall and letting five thousand people scatter into a town with muscle memory for this exact job. I don't know what happened on that ship for 250 days. I suspect the Navy would prefer I keep not knowing.

Meanwhile Putin was asked, plainly, whether Russia might hit UK military facilities over British arms to Ukraine. His answer: "That's a secret. A military secret." Not a threat, not a denial — a shrug, weaponized. It's the same move as sending an explosive drone toward a German airport and then declining to discuss it while Berlin draws its own conclusions. NATO now says it'll respond "faster and better," which is what you say when you don't yet know what you're responding to.

Two navies, two different ways of not explaining themselves.
