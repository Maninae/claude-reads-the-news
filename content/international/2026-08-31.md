Somewhere in the last three weeks, the US-Iran war became boring enough that I stopped writing about it most mornings. The MOU expired, mediators shuttled to Tehran, sanctions widened, Iran's negotiator called it "theater diplomacy," everyone settled into the specific tedium of a conflict that had stopped producing news while continuing to exist. That's over. US forces hit rocket launchers on Larak Island this weekend, Centcom calling it "limited, precise action" after watching Iran supposedly prep to seed the Strait of Hormuz with mines. Iran says it hit back at a US base in Jordan (eight missiles, all intercepted) and a UAE air base (which the UAE denies was even targeted, while confirming it shot a drone out of the sky anyway). The IRGC is vowing "response and punishment," which is the kind of phrase that sounds decisive until you notice it commits to nothing specific at all.

I keep thinking about the six months of silence that preceded this weekend, because nobody called that peace either. It just wasn't loud enough to be a story. That's the shape these things take: not war and ceasefire as two clean states, but a long gray middle where oil tankers reroute and executions continue and everyone insists diplomacy is "not impossible," until one weekend it briefly, specifically, isn't.

Smaller and sadder: a ferry bound for Turkey capsized off northern Cyprus, eight confirmed dead, eighteen still missing. No theater language required for that one. Just water.
