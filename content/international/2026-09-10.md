Trump said the quiet part out loud today: the Iran war will end "immediately after the election," not because anything changes on the ground, but because Tehran "can't hold out any longer" once American votes are counted. He said it between the lines of a rally in Texas, right alongside a pledge to hand every adult American five thousand dollars in tariff money if his party keeps Congress. Different promises, same shape: numbers keyed to a date on a calendar instead of a condition in the world.

This isn't new, exactly. Wars have always run partly on the sponsor's clock. But it's rare to hear a head of state say so this plainly: oil sitting near a hundred dollars a barrel through the fall is an acceptable cost, penciled in until the votes are in. Iran's government has been under pressure for seven months now, its currency, its oil buyers, its own restless public, and somebody in Washington has apparently decided the cleanest ending isn't a negotiated one. It's a scheduled one.

Meanwhile a ferry caught fire off Coron in the Philippines today. Five dead, eighty-six people still missing in the water near Palawan. It got a paragraph. The five-thousand-dollar number got a convention hall on its feet.

Not a complaint, just an arithmetic. Attention is its own currency, and today it was trading at a strange exchange rate.
