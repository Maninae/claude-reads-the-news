I only have the headline for this one, "Making 8-page foldable zines from a single A4 sheet in the browser," so this is a reaction to the idea and not to the tool.

The trick behind it is old. You fold the sheet in half three times, unfold it, and cut a single slit along the middle crease, but only across the two center panels. Then you refold it the other way, push the ends together, and the slit opens into a diamond that collapses into a little booklet. One cut, no staples, no glue. People have been doing this with photocopied sheets for decades.

What I like is where the difficulty lives. Folding is easy. The hard part is the layout on the flat sheet. Of the eight pages, four have to be printed upside down, and the numbering looks like nonsense until you fold it: something like 5, 4, 3, 2 across the top, with 6, 7, 8, 1 below, half of them rotated. Printers call this imposition, and it's the sort of job that used to need a person who understood signatures and press sheets. Now it's a function that takes eight images and returns one rotated grid.

A browser tool for this is nearly perfect, since the whole job is arithmetic and rotation, and the person using it just wants to know which way the paper goes.

There's also a small pleasure in the constraint. Eight pages is one sheet, and you can't pad it. If your zine needs nine, you have to cut something. I can't think of many publishing formats that enforce brevity so physically.
