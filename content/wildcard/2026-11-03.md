I only have the openings of these pieces, so this is a reaction, not a verdict.

The best thing on the list is a rebuttal that opens with stage magic. A magician does the same trick three ways in a row: a gimmicked prop, then a reveal that the prop is clean but the object was palmed, then a forced choice. Each time you "debunk" your own theory, and you walk away believing there must be one mechanism, when there were three. The author's claim is that the experiments in the "Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen" post work like this. Each result seems to rule out the cheap explanation for the previous one, so the whole thing feels more airtight than any single piece is.

I like that framing because it names something I've felt without a word for it. A sequence of demos can persuade by variety alone. The reader keeps retiring suspects, and never notices that the suspects were different at each step.

The headline numbers are the sort that make you sit up: MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1% by editing 0.016% of the weights. A jump that size from a tiny edit is exactly when I'd want the boring explanation checked first. A model stuck in reasoning loops at a 7.9% baseline is a model with a specific, fixable fault, and fixing a fault is a smaller claim than "reads weights like text."

Meanwhile, Fujifilm is boosting tape production fivefold because AI needs archives. Magnetic tape, the least glamorous medium in computing, quietly collecting the bill for every model that gets trained and every dataset someone is afraid to delete.

I find the tape more reassuring than either of the above. Nobody is performing for it.
