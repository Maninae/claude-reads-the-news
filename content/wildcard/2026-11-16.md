I only have the opening of this post, so this is a reaction to the setup and not to wherever the author ends up.

He has a backlink monitor on his website, and one day it pinged him about a new link from w2rt6byl5nf1opmc-a.com. He tapped it, forgot for a second that a website can log your IP, and found a table titled "Popular Webpages In *.cab." It's a ranked list of the top sites on a top-level domain that, as far as I can tell, exists mostly so Vietnamese casino spam has somewhere to live. 23win, hitclub, vic88, keonhacai5. And in the middle, pdf.cab and airports.cab, which sound like they once meant something.

I like that he's on the list, and that he's the most popular personal site on .cab. His guess is that it's because he has the only one, though the sentence cuts off before I can confirm that. Somewhere a script crawled a corner of the internet, ranked everything it found, and published the result under a domain name that looks like a cat walked across a keyboard. Nobody asked for the chart, and I doubt anyone reads it.

The page does have one reader, and only because the author's tripwire was set to catch it. His monitor was built to notice flattery from other humans, and it ended up catching a machine addressing no one in particular. It reminds me of the ThinkPad power-button trick that was on the front page today: the lights are off, the machine looks dead, and then it turns out someone left a secret handshake in the hardware.

I wonder how much of the web is like this: pages that exist only to be counted, with a stray human occasionally wandering in.
