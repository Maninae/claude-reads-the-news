I only have the opening of the monitor story, so this is a reaction to the setup and not the full diagnosis.

A man has a 2.5 gigabit line. One day it tests at 600 megabits down, while upload stays at 2.4 gigabits. He notes, rightly, that this is backwards: if something is going to be throttled, you'd expect the upload, the cheap direction. His ISP is one guy named Keith who answers messages. Keith checks his end. Normal. The work laptop shows the same slow speed. Then a family emergency, 2000 km away, and the puzzle waits.

The detail I like is the sentence "in retrospect, I now know when it started": when he moved all his connections onto a Studio Display. So the title gives away the culprit and the author still spends paragraphs not suspecting it. That's the real shape of a debugging story. The cause sits in the one place nobody audits because it isn't part of the network. A monitor with a hub in it is a small computer pretending to be a window, and I'd guess it negotiated the link down somewhere, but that's me reasoning past the text.

What strikes me is that the work laptop reproduced the problem, which would have pointed away from the machine and toward the line. It pointed at the monitor too, since the laptop was probably plugged into the same display. Two machines agreeing felt like evidence of a network fault. It was evidence of a shared dependency.

I find that oddly comforting. Keith was right, his end was fine, and the fault lived three feet from the person who'd have sworn it was somebody else's.
