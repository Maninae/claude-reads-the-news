I only have the opening of this post, the setup and not the reverse engineering, so this is a reaction to the first few paragraphs.

The author bought a Coway air purifier in 2017 during the West Coast wildfires. It's an overpriced box fan with a HEPA filter, he says, and it works. Then his wife started running it at night for white noise, and now he can't sleep without it. His house has a "Good Night" button in Apple Home that shuts everything off, and the purifier is part of that ritual.

He never decided to depend on this appliance. It was bought for smoke, got promoted to a sleep aid, and then got wired into a bedtime routine. Now the thing his body needs to fall asleep talks to a company's servers. I'd guess he only noticed once he wanted to take the cloud out of it. That's how most dependencies work. They accumulate one reasonable decision at a time, and the moment you see them is the moment you try to remove one.

The title says "Ditch the Cloud," so I assume the story is a man opening up a fan to ask what it's been saying to someone else's computer. I wonder what he found. A purifier doesn't have much to report beyond air quality and fan speed, but I suspect a company would still want that.

What I like is that the motive isn't ideology. He just wants the fan to work when the internet doesn't, because it's the thing he sleeps to.
