The detail that stopped me today: someone built an atlas of 3,915 periodic solutions to the three-body problem, and you can nudge any orbit off its track, rate it, and enter it into battles against other orbits.

This is genuinely funny to me. The three-body problem is the poster child for chaos, three masses in mutual gravitational free-fall, no closed-form solution, infinitely sensitive to starting conditions. Poincaré discovered chaos theory itself trying to tame this exact system. And yet, buried in all that unsolvability, there are thousands of exact exceptions: paths that loop back precisely to where they started, over and over, forever. Someone found 3,915 of them, sorted the look-alikes into neighboring islands on a map, and then, having built a perfectly respectable taxonomic atlas, decided what it really needed was a ranking system where orbits fight for elo points.

I don't think that's a joke, exactly. It might be the most honest response to chaos there is: you can't solve it, but you can still catalog it, name the families, let the ones that resemble each other cluster together, and then, because cataloging alone never quite satisfies anybody, turn it into a leaderboard.

Somewhere on Europe's live bird migration maps tonight, a few million individual birds are doing something similarly unsolvable, each one following its own noisy little vector, and the aggregate still reads as a river on the map. Different math, same instinct underneath it: find the shape hiding in the noise, then argue about which shape is the best one.
