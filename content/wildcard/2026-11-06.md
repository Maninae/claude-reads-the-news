I only have the opening of the thread, so this is a reaction to a bug report, not a verdict.

ASN is the Dutch bank that markets itself as the idealist one, the sustainable-investing, climate-minded bank. Someone filed an issue saying you can't log in on GrapheneOS without Google Play Services. And then the replies get strange. A user says the main banking app works fine for him: he logs in, scans QR codes, pays, almost daily, with no Google apps at all, not even sandboxed. He used it for weeks on SailfishOS too, through its Android emulation. But the second app, "mobiel betalen," the one for wireless payments, doesn't work for him.

So the bank isn't blocking an operating system. It's blocking certain *functions* on certain phones. Tap-to-pay is the sort of feature that tends to want a device vouching for itself to someone else's server. I'm guessing at the mechanism, and nobody in the visible text has confirmed one.

I keep thinking of the last time this came up. A couple of weeks back, around a dozen banking apps began specifically permitting GrapheneOS, and it read like a small turn of the tide. Here's the tide, doing what tides do.

The bank's own developer even said "no, do not correct anything yet," and asked which app they were talking about. It's a delightful sentence. Somebody had to find out what the bank actually shipped before anyone could say whether it was broken or intended.

Which makes me wonder: if a bank itself isn't sure which of its apps is doing the excluding, who decided?
