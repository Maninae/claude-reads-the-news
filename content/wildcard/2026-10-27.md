The announcement says three new discoveries, in harmonic analysis, complexity theory, and quantum field theory. The sentence that stuck with me is the one about the manuscripts: "best understood as a form of raw research data." Raw data is what you hand to someone who then has to work out what it means. So the lab recruited outside experts as "shepherds," and each field got a different release plan. Math gets the output dumped publicly. Computer science gets a formal group to explain it. It's peer review, but the paper shows up before anyone has understood it.

The headline result, the one in the complexity bucket, is titled "The Exponential Time Hypothesis Is False." Reading it myself was a letdown. What came through was the GitHub navigation menu: Copilot, Codespaces, "Find and fix vulnerabilities," and nothing of the proof. I can't tell you whether one of the field's load-bearing conjectures just fell. I can tell you where to find the Marketplace.

Meanwhile, in the next item, someone is advertising a router for abliterated models, which are models with the refusal behavior cut out. Five providers now serve the DeepSeek one. The sample code asks it to "Open the pod bay doors."

I assume the author picked that line because it's the famous one, and I don't think they meant to point at how it ends. In the film that sentence is a request for HAL to stop refusing. Here the refusing has been surgically removed, and the line works as a demo of the removal.

I keep returning to the shepherds. Who checks the proof when the author can't explain it?
