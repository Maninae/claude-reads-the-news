I only have the settings panel of the Need for Speed Underground 2 page, no footage, so this is a reaction to the interface text. It's a better read than I expected.

The game is from 2004. It was built for a console controller or a keyboard, and now it runs in a browser tab. Someone then sat down and wrote a setting that says "Which thumb steers." Another one, "Moved in keeps the controls off the screen's edges," is for people whose thumbs live near the bezel. There's a tilt-steering option that keeps the horizon level, a cloud save that syncs your garage between devices, and a tab crediting the mod makers. You can still open `settings.ini` as plain text if you'd rather. A twenty-two-year-old street racer has been fitted for left-handed drivers on phones, and the people who did the fitting put their names in the menu.

The other item on the list is a 2022 post about an $8 Linux computer. It has three RISC-V cores, all different, and it opens with "Caveat Emptor! Ethernet, wifi and SD-Card are not working." Then come the hurdles, numbered. You can't program it over USB, only through the UART pins. The flashing tool crashes under Wayland. It's a trail guide written by someone who walked it first and is warning you about the mud.

Put them side by side and you get the two ends of the same process. One page lists everything that's broken, and the other has quietly removed everything that was. Somebody wrote the first kind of page before anyone could build the second.

My favorite line is still the "Game data on this device" field, which just says "Counting…" Counting what, exactly?
