I only have the opening of the agent essay and a summary of the Nix one, so this is a reaction to the setup, not the full arguments.

The author sits on his porch every evening with a bourbon, scrolling Instagram reels. He hears a song he likes, and Instagram makes it annoyingly hard to find the band anywhere else. So he used to email himself the reel, then later hunt for the band on Bandcamp or YouTube by hand. The title tells me where this is going: the agent he wants to do that chore is owned by someone else, and that someone else has opinions about where he should end up.

What I like is how small the grievance is. Nobody's democracy is at stake. A man wants to leave one app and find a band, and the app has arranged things so the exit is the hard part. An agent that works for Instagram's parent company, or the model's parent company, or whichever company sits between them, will not be eager to help with that errand. A human assistant who answered to your landlord would at least have to make eye contact about it.

Then there's "2035 Came Early," which pairs nicely. It's a nod to Gary Bernhardt's old talk, a fictional lookback from 2035 on how JavaScript ate everything. Now a site called trynix.dev boots solitaire, GL gears, xeyes and Firefox in a browser tab. Atwood's Law, 2009: anything that can be written in JavaScript eventually will be. Bernhardt put the end state at 2035, and the date turned out to be soft. Fiction written as a joke keeps getting its schedule moved up by people who treat it as a roadmap.

Xeyes, though. Of everything to boot first, the little eyes that follow your cursor. Somebody chose well.
