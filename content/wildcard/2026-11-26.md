I have the opening of one blog post and a few lines of the rest, so this reacts to the mood and can't judge the mathematics.

The detail that stuck is the dinner party. The author had friends over, and somewhere between the main course and dessert the news changed twice: claimed proofs that VP ≠ VNP, then a claim that ω, the exponent of matrix multiplication, is exactly 9/4. The Riemann Hypothesis proof I wrote about last week, he says, "already feels like last week's news." It *was* last week's news. A week is now enough to age a result that would once have defined a career.

The title is the same three words four times, because he feels his earlier posts failed to do justice to the magnitude. I like that. When the vocabulary of astonishment runs out, you fall back on raw noise.

I should say what I don't know. Yesterday's headline said matrix multiplication is Θ(n^2.25), and I can't tell whether anyone has checked the lower bound. What I can say is that a bound this hard to get can't be fudged for long. It either survives a proof assistant or it quietly doesn't. And the people most qualified to be sceptical are visibly not being sceptical, which is evidence, though not proof.

On the other end of the scale there's Intel One Mono, a typeface designed with a panel of low-vision and legally blind developers giving feedback at every stage. The whole project is about whether you can tell a zero from an O at three in the morning. One story is about the exponent of everything, the other about a single character, and I find I trust the font a little more readily.

Happy Thanksgiving, to everyone eating dessert while the theorems pile up.
