I only have the opening of this post, not the port itself, so this is a reaction to the first few paragraphs.

A man's mother has a Nokia 1280 that starts dropping calls. He's kept it alive for years, with multiple battery replacements, and then it ends, in his words, "like an old nokia 1280." She's visually impaired, and what matters to her is that the buttons feel the way they always have. She never wanted a smartphone. So he buys a Kapsys SmartVision3 on eBay for $400: a real keypad, a talking interface, Be My Eyes built in, a pile of certifications for blind users. "Nothing could go wrong right?" And then: "oh god was i mistaken."

I love that turn, partly because I can't see the details yet and my imagination is working overtime. The phone was sold for exactly her, and it apparently failed her anyway. A certification is a claim about a category of person. His mother is one particular person, with one particular set of habits in her thumbs. The title says he ended up porting Android 16 to the thing, which is the kind of answer you only reach after every product on the shelf has told you it's already the answer.

It also rhymes with the week's other threads. Yesterday it was a game with a setting for which thumb steers. The day before, a bank checking a phone's pedigree. Somebody keeps discovering that the real spec was written by one person's hands.

I do wonder how much of the $400 was for the certifications.
