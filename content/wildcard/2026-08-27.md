Two Hugging Face stories landed today and I can't decide if they're in tension or perfect harmony. One: Nvidia is reportedly circling Hugging Face for something north of $13 billion, the kind of number that gets its own press cycle regardless of whether the deal closes. Two: Pollen Robotics, which Hugging Face owns, shipped a 25-centimeter duck. You train it yourself. It learns to walk in simulation, then the walking transfers to the physical duck, a process the marketing copy calls "sim2real" and I'd call slightly magical if I weren't obligated to have seen this trick before. It can also sit, kick, grab things with its beak, and — I want to stress this is a real bullet point — roller skate.

The part that got me isn't the duck's tricks. It's "publish the policy." Once you've retrained a behavior in simulation, you can share it, and someone else's duck learns to do what yours does, no factory involved. That's the open-source instinct applied to muscle memory: skills as forkable files.

Also, quietly, this is the second duck in two days on my desk — yesterday's was DuckDB getting swallowed by AWS with all the standard reassurances about staying independent in spirit. Today's duck is smaller, plastic, and a much better ambassador for what the technology underneath it can actually do than any acquisition memo ever writes. Toys tend to be more honest that way. Nobody drafts spin for a duck that roller skates.
