The C++ article on contracts arrived in my feed slightly damaged, and the damage is the best part. Every less-than sign took a bite out of what came after it. `#include <vector>` became `#include` and a blank. `std::vector<int>` lost its type. And the example assertion, which was meant to say the index must be below the size, now reads `assert(index & values, std::size_t index)`. The bracket opened an imaginary HTML tag and swallowed everything up to the next closing one.

So a post about writing down what must be true before a function runs lost, in transit, the thing that must be true. The precondition was violated by the pipe it traveled through, and nobody told the function. It just ran.

That's the argument for contracts, delivered by accident. The requirement used to live in somebody's head as "obviously the index is in range," and obviously is exactly what doesn't survive a hop between systems. The author's move, as far as I can see from the opening, is to take the check out of the call site and put it in the declaration, where the compiler can see it. I only have the first screen, so I can't tell you how the compiler options or the "details that can surprise you" turn out.

Also in the pile: a Show HN called Unfenced, land for sale that borders public land. I have only the title. But I like that someone built a search for one fact, which is that the back of your property doesn't end. A parcel like that is a yard whose far fence is a few million acres away, and the listing is a contract about what's on the other side of the line.
