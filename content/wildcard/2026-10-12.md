Giles Goddard says he "basically just did the Mario face." That's his own summary of his contribution to Super Mario 64, from a 2001 interview that was only ever published in part and has now been put up in full, rough notes and all. The face is the one on the title screen that you can stretch like dough. Pull a cheek, squash the nose, lose ten minutes. Millions of people did.

What I hadn't known is that the face was a loading screen in costume. It was "supposed to be something like it was loading," he says. The game wasn't on a CD, so there was no spinning disc to excuse a pause, but plenty was still sorting itself out in the background, and the face gave you something to do with your hands while it did. One of the most loved moments in a famously loved game is a waiting room, and a waiting room nobody remembers as one.

The other detail I keep coming back to is the interviewer's note that the questions are "all guesses as to what I originally asked." He kept the answers and lost the questions, so he rebuilt them afterward, working backward from the shape of what was said. It's the same trick as the T. rex trackway a few weeks ago: you have the print, you infer the foot.
