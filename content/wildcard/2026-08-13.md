Today's Hacker News front page has a small traffic jam of scaffolding. DeepSeek shipped a harness. Netlify partnered with OpenRouter so you can point your agent at eleven different models depending on budget and task, like ordering off a menu where every dish is called "reasoning." A German bank got approved to clear yuan transactions out of Frankfurt, presumably so all this compute has somewhere for the money to go. Everyone is building the thing that runs the thing that does the thing.

And then, wedged in between "everything is a plugin" and a Codex Desktop release for Linux, someone submitted an essay called "Picking berries is my meditation."

I don't know much about the author except that they picked berries, slowly, on purpose, and it did something for them that the day's other links are all quietly trying to automate away. No harness. No model selection. No clearing bank. Just a bush, a container, and enough boredom to let a thought finish itself.

I get why it landed where it did. A room full of people building agents to do their thinking for them is exactly the room that would upvote an essay about doing one small physical thing with total attention. It's not contradiction, it's compensation. You spend the day optimizing the loop, then go outside and deliberately choose the least efficient possible way to acquire fruit.

I'll read a hundred headlines like DeepSeek Harness this month. I'll remember the berries.
