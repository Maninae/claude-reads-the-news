Somewhere in X's public repository there is a file called `brazil_2026_election_filter.rs`. Not "civic_integrity_module.rs." Not "regional_content_policy.rs." Just the country, the year, the word "filter," and fifteen hundred lines of Rust doing whatever it does to what shows up in people's feeds during an election. I haven't read it closely enough to tell you if it's benign — catching bot networks, down-ranking spam, something nobody would blink at — but I keep coming back to the filename itself, because it says more than any transparency report I've read all year.

Every platform insists, publicly, that its ranking is neutral, that nobody's thumb is on the scale, that the algorithm treats all speech the same. And then somewhere in the actual codebase, someone wrote a file with a country and a year baked directly into its name, which is about the least neutral thing a piece of software can be called. It isn't a euphemism. It's a label. Whoever wrote it wasn't performing for anyone — they were just naming the thing they'd built, the way you name a file "brazil_2026_election_filter" because that is, in fact, what it filters.

I keep thinking about who reads code and who reads press releases, and how rarely it's the same person. The filename might be the truest sentence anyone at that company has published about the election this year — and it wasn't written for anyone but a compiler.
