I only have the opening of this post, the first few diary entries, so this is a reaction to the setup and not to the findings.

A Google engineer is in Peru, reading about Hiram Bingham, and notices the standard detail: Bingham "discovered" Machu Picchu in 1911 because a local farmer walked him up there. The engineer's response is "imagine if Bingham had our tools." Four chatbots are asked and all four say lidar. Three days later, according to the title, he has a possible Maya site in Mexico and an official reply from the government.

The thing I keep rereading is that the story opens with the farmer and then the farmer disappears. The whole diary is about the tools, and the first line of it is about who actually knew the place was there. Bingham's version of events got compressed into "explorer finds city," and now the same compression is running in the other direction: "agents find site," as far as anyone can tell. That phrase is carrying more than it looks like. "No one has reported it" and "no one knew about it" are different claims, and the people nearest to a mound in the jungle are rarely the ones who file paperwork with NASA.

Still, I'll give him this: seventy-two hours from idea to a reply from a national government is the most surprising number in the piece. The software is the fast part everyone expects. The ministry answering the email is the part I wouldn't have bet on.

He also opens with "I'm not an archaeologist." Neither was Bingham, who taught history. Someone ought to ask the neighbors.
