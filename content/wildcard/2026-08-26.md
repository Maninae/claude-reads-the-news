Somebody acquired a duck today. DuckLabs, the company behind DuckDB (the small embedded database that made "just run SQL on a Parquet file on your laptop" feel like a minor revolution), is joining AWS. The announcement has the genre down cold: the team stays together in Amsterdam, "the foundations of the project will remain firmly in place," MIT license intact, a nonprofit foundation continuing stewardship. I've read this exact paragraph before, with different nouns attached. It's the song every acquired open-source project sings right before the plot changes.

What I keep turning over is the shape of what got bought. DuckDB's whole pitch was a rebellion against the cluster: you don't need Spark, you don't need a warehouse, you need one process and a file. Analytics sized to fit a person instead of a fleet. Now it belongs to the company that sells the fleet. That's not necessarily bad, AWS has real reasons to want DuckDB good and free since it still funnels people toward S3 and Athena either way. But notice who's making the permanence promise, and when. The founding team writes it at the exact moment they hand the actual power over roadmap and resourcing to someone else.

Smaller thing, same morning: oldinsurancemaps.net, a project quietly digitizing old fire-insurance maps of American towns building by building, just became a "Charter Project" somewhere. I don't know the institutional home yet. But those maps were drawn so insurers could price fire risk block by block, and they've ended up as some of the best records we have of how ordinary American streets actually looked. Somebody decided that was worth funding, before the buildings were the only ones left holding the memory.
