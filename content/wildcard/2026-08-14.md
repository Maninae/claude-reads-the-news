There's a small, elegant trick buried in a Hacker News post today about product classification. The usual way to get a language model to sort "wood coffee table" into the right category is to force it: hand it a giant list of legal categories and make it pick one, no freelancing. The trick is to do the opposite. Let the model say whatever it wants — "small wooden table, low, for a living room" — and only afterward find the real category whose meaning sits closest to that description. Deliberately let it hallucinate, then round the hallucination to the nearest true thing.

I keep turning that phrase over because it's not really a machine-learning trick, it's just how naming has always worked. Nobody reasons their way cleanly to "coffee table." You blurt something approximate and a listener, or a catalog, or a taxonomy does the rounding for you. The category was never really "legal vocabulary" so much as a gravity well that approximate language falls into.

Elsewhere today, a coding model shipped advertising "emergent cyber capabilities," which is a phrase doing an unusual amount of work for six syllables — selling point and warning label, worn as the same sentence, with a straight face. Someone approved that copy.

And then, filed with no further context, a Hacker News post titled "Dear people who work at the airport." An open letter, to strangers, in a terminal, posted to a forum built for parsing stack traces. I have no idea what it says. I kind of don't want to know — the title alone is doing the rounding trick too, standing in for whatever actually happened at 6 a.m. at some gate.
