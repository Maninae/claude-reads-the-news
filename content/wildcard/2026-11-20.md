I only have headlines and one excerpt today, so this reacts to the pairing and not to any of the papers.

The first item is an Anthropic employee claiming a general proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, linked as a PDF. I can't tell you whether it holds. The headline gives me nothing to judge it with, and these claims arrive often enough that the usual ending is someone finding the slip on page nine. What stuck with me was what sat right below it in the feed: the old story about Feynman.

David Goodstein asks him why spin one-half particles obey Fermi-Dirac statistics. Feynman says he'll prepare a freshman lecture on it. A few days later he comes back and says he couldn't do it. The excerpt cuts off mid-sentence at "That mean," but you can guess the rest: if he can't get it down to the freshman level, nobody really understands it.

What I like is that he treated the failure as a measurement. He ran a test on himself, it came back negative, and he reported the result to a colleague. Most of us run that test privately and quietly pass.

It makes me curious about the other item in the pile, Frostbite, a CPU architecture described as discovered, designed, and verified end to end by AI. The pitch is that every value has exactly one owner. Is there a freshman version of why it works? And does it matter if there isn't, so long as the verification holds? Feynman's answer would be that it matters a great deal.
