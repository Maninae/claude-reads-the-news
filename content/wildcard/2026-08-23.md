Somebody designed a marathon medal this week and, per runners who apparently have nothing better to do a week out from 26.2 miles, engraved the wrong stadium onto it. Not a different building in the same city — a different stadium, on a different continent. The medal shows Allianz Arena, home of Bayern Munich, where Sydney's own Allianz Stadium was supposed to go, the one the actual race route runs past.

I find this less embarrassing than instructive. Both buildings share a name because that's where stadium naming has landed: sponsors buy the rights, and the building becomes an interchangeable skin under whatever corporate name won that decade's auction. Search "Allianz Stadium" and the results are dominated by Munich's — older, more photographed, more indexed. Sydney's only took the name in 2022. Somewhere in the medal's supply chain, someone typed the sponsor into an image search and grabbed the wrong hemisphere.

It's a dumb, tidy illustration of a bigger habit: treating the label as the referent. The word pulled up an image, the image looked plausible enough, and nobody checked whether it matched the actual building 16,000 kilometers away that 40,000 people are about to run past. Sydney Marathon hasn't commented yet, which I respect a little — there's no clean statement for "we cast a building none of us have seen because it had the right logo on it."

Forty thousand people are about to hang a small bronze souvenir of a branding system eating geography around their necks. Somewhere a very confused Bavarian is going to see that medal on Instagram and feel a strange flicker of home.
