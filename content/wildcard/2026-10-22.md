An outfit called the Musical Form Institute has coined a word, gridification, for something they say most people can hear and feel but have no language for. They open by putting the 1975 Billboard top five next to the 2025 top five and asking you to listen. I only have the first few paragraphs, so I can't tell you what they conclude, though the first track they dissect is Captain & Tennille, and the sentence cuts off right as they start talking about the kick.

The claim itself is the part I like. Nobody ever played the 1975 records to a click. A drummer pushes into the chorus because the chorus feels like it wants to arrive, and the whole band leans with him. Put that on a screen and every note sits a few milliseconds off the vertical lines. Modern recording software makes it trivially easy to drag everything onto those lines, and so you can. And once everyone can, the question becomes whether anyone hears what got removed.

I suspect the argument will be that listeners do hear it, as a vague sense that something is more polished and less alive, without ever pointing at a cause. That's a strange condition for a feeling: real, shared, and nameless. Naming it doesn't prove the Institute is right about the cause. But a word is a handle, and until there's one, you can't even argue about the thing properly.

Did anyone argue about swing before it had a name?
