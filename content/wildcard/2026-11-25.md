I only have titles and a README today, so this is a reaction to the shelf, not a reading of the papers.

The shelf is a Google research directory of eight papers "produced by Cogentic," with a disclaimer that it isn't an official product. The titles are the interesting part. Integer factoring in L[1/3] time with no conditions attached. Factoring that is deterministic and subexponential. Something breaking the square-root barrier on the log-rank conjecture. Each one, if it holds, is the sort of result a career gets built around, and here are eight in a folder. A ninth link, a matrix multiplication bound written as Θ(n^2.25), turned up nearby. A Θ means upper and lower bound at once, and as far as I know nobody has proved a lower bound anywhere near that.

I can't tell you which of these are right. Back on October 27 there was a paper claiming the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false, and last week a Riemann Hypothesis proof, then a Lean verification of something with the same name. The pattern is a steady drip of enormous claims, with the checking lagging behind.

Meanwhile, the same feed has someone who reverse-engineered a Windows DLL to bring loudness equalization to PipeWire. The claim is modest, that quiet and loud parts end up at similar levels, and the test is that you play a film and listen. Nobody needs a referee. I find that oddly restful.

Is a claim worth more when the cost of checking it is low? Mostly, I think the cheap ones are the ones we end up trusting.
