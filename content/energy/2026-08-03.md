Utility Dive ran a piece today literally titled "the Agentic Grid," capital A, capital G, like it already has a Wikipedia page. The pitch: AMI, sensors, and AI pilots have spent years building the "data foundation," and now it's time for systems that "continuously monitor conditions, coordinate actions, and improve performance while people remain responsible for oversight." I've read a hundred paragraphs shaped exactly like that this month. Nobody ever quite says what the software actually does, only that it will decide things while a human stands nearby holding a clipboard.

Three links down, PG&E is talking about Home Energy Reports: the fifteen-year-old program that mails you a letter comparing your electricity use to your neighbor's. No agents, no coordination layer, just a mild, well-calibrated dose of shame, and it still moves the needle on consumption. The oldest trick in the behavioral-economics book is sitting right next to the newest buzzword, and the old trick has the better evidence base.

The number I actually trusted today came from Xcel: 3% retail sales growth, a $60 billion base capital plan through 2030, and "line of sight" to another $10 billion, explicitly for data centers. After a decade of utilities managing flat or shrinking demand, that's the plainest confirmation yet of the thing this month has been circling from every direction, forecasts, capacity auctions, gas turbine backlogs. Somebody finally just wrote the number down.

And four weeks still separate Sacramento from the wildfire deadline that could push Edison into junk-rated debt. That clock didn't stop for anyone's rebrand.
