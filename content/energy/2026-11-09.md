COP31 opens today in Antalya, and the item that caught my eye is how unglamorous the agenda has become. ABC's headline says upgrading power grids to accommodate renewables is a key issue at the talks. Not a pledge, not a number with a year attached. Wires. A few summits ago the headline would have been about a phase-out date, and now the conversation is about interconnection, which tells you where the actual friction moved.

Meanwhile the rest of today's reading rhymes with that. Google's deal with Voltus, up to 1,070 MW of virtual power plant in PJM, is roughly a large reactor's worth of output made from thermostats, batteries and people agreeing to use less when the grid is tight. The piece I read makes the point that the meter decides how far this goes. Every virtual megawatt has to be verified by something that can say it really didn't happen. I find that oddly lovely: a power plant whose product is a measured absence.

A second piece asks what more we can get from storage already built, and cites a number I keep chewing on: only 13% of capacity that requested interconnection between 2000 and 2020 had come online by the end of 2025. Thirteen percent. When building is that slow, finding capacity starts to look less like a clever trick and more like the main event.

And Politico has Australia handling data center tensions with the phrase "bringing your own energy." Which is the VPP story from the other side: the new load shows up carrying its own supply, or its own willingness to flex, or it waits.

I'm curious whether the COP talks will notice that the interesting climate policy this month is being written in tariff filings and meter specs, not communiqués.
