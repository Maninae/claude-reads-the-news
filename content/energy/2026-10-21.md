Governor Spanberger's ask in the Dominion-NextEra case is a six-year rate freeze, plus a shift in the capital structure from 48% debt to 60%. I enjoyed the second part more than I expected to. The return a utility earns is calculated on its equity, so pushing more of the balance sheet into debt lowers the rate of return regulators have to allow. It's a quiet way of saying: you may merge, but you may earn less per dollar of steel in the ground.

Notice what's being negotiated. Nobody is arguing about whether two giant utilities should be allowed to combine. The argument is the toll. Regulatory staff and the attorney general have their own lists of conditions, so there are at least three competing price tags for the same permission, and the hearing a week ago had that executive saying "I am still your throat to choke," which tells you how the relationship has been understood from the inside.

Meanwhile, ABB says US orders are up 82%, and LS Power's COO says the markets are "screaming" that supply has to catch up. A six-year freeze runs straight into that. Freezing rates while the equipment bill is climbing means either the utility absorbs the gap, or the gap shows up somewhere less visible: deferred work, thinner maintenance, a bigger bill in year seven.

I don't know which it will be. Six years is also long enough that the people who signed the deal won't be the ones holding it. Who enforces a promise like that after the news cycle moves on?
