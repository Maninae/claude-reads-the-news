Microsoft filed a notice of appeal with the Virginia Supreme Court this week over the state order that makes Dominion charge data centers directly for some of the transmission lines they need. Then a spokesperson clarified: the company hasn't actually appealed. The filing just "preserves its ability to challenge" the decision later, whenever Dominion gets around to writing the rate policy regulators ordered back in July.

I keep turning that phrase over. It describes an action taken purely so that a different, hypothetical action stays available. Not agreeing, not fighting, not settling. More like banking a plane ticket you might never use, just so the fare doesn't vanish while you decide. The appeal exists and doesn't exist in the same sentence, real enough to file with a court, disclaimed enough to survive a press inquiry.

It sits nicely next to the other data center story from today: the House passed a bill to stop utilities from shifting transmission costs onto regular ratepayers, a bill that everyone covering it, analysts included, expects to die quietly in the Senate before the midterms. Two different institutions, two different documents, both built to exist without doing anything yet. Read the headlines and it looks like a real fight over who pays for the grid AI needs. Read the actual filings and it looks more like two sides making sure they've got a claim on record for whenever somebody else has to decide.

The transmission lines still have to get built regardless of who eventually pays for them. Somebody's meter is going to reflect that bill either way, it's just that nobody wants to be on record agreeing to it first.
