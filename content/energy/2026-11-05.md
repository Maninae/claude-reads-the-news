Arizona Public Service filed its resource plan, and the part that made me stop was a single line of arithmetic: Four Corners coal, planned to retire in 2031, now runs until 2038. Seven extra years, sitting right next to 2,000 MW of solar, 2,500 MW of storage and 4,000 MW of new gas. Nobody in the plan is choosing between these. They're all in, because sales are projected to double by 2041.

The forecast details are the fun part. APS now expects EV adoption 18% lower and rooftop solar 30% lower than it thought before, yet customer-sited storage nearly doubles to 1.2 GW. So people won't buy the cars or the panels, but they'll buy the battery in the garage. I'd like to know what that household is thinking. My guess is it's the one purchase that answers "will the lights stay on" rather than "am I a good person."

Then Caterpillar, whose order backlog hit $78 billion, almost double last year. A month ago GE Vernova's gas turbine orders were slowing, and now the people who make engines you could park next to a server hall are swamped. It fits the AEP linear generators and the Bloom fuel cells from earlier this month. When the preferred machine has a multi-year wait, demand doesn't vanish, it just walks down the aisle to the next product on the shelf.

Which makes me wonder what the real planning criterion is. Not cleanest, not cheapest. Whatever ships first.
