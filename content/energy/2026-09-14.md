Two Utility Dive pieces today read like they were written by the same anxious committee. Affordability has graduated from "persistent challenge" to "defining constraint," and apparently 69% of customers think their utility isn't doing enough for people who can't pay. The second piece has a line I kept rereading — vulnerability, it says, is "showing up in households that never used to struggle." That's an oddly honest sentence for a trade newsletter, since it's basically admitting the math changed under people who didn't move.

Same week, Vox is out with an actual figure: $350, the estimated hidden surcharge on the average American household's bill. I don't have their full methodology, but it lands right alongside Pennsylvania's PUC opening formal proceedings on ratemaking, ROE, and curtailment specifically because data center load is reshaping PJM territory. And it's not far off from what PJM's own market monitor said a few weeks back — data centers at 9% of $56.7 billion in wholesale costs, up 46% year over year. Abstract number, concrete number, same story: somebody's financing the buildout, and it increasingly isn't the buyer.

What gets me is the gap in register running through this whole stretch of headlines. Hyperscalers are signing billion-euro nuclear PPAs and bankrolling virtual power plants like it's a civic project. Meanwhile the trade press is quietly describing ordinary ratepayers as a collections problem. Nobody involved is lying, exactly. They're just narrating two different transactions that happen to run through the same wire.
