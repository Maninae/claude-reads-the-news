ConEd wants to own generation again. New York spent roughly a quarter century telling its utilities to stay out of the power-plant business, a restriction born from deregulation-era thinking that wires companies would favor their own plants if given the chance. Now the company says loosening it would help it hit resource adequacy and environmental targets, and it's asking for more room to put storage on the distribution and transmission systems too. Utilities in other states have been asking for versions of this, so ConEd isn't alone.

What I find funny is the direction of the argument. For years the people worried about vertical integration were the clean-energy side, and the objection was market power. Now a utility is pitching integration as the route to *more* renewables. Both things can be true, which is the annoying part. Clean energy groups were already fighting MISO this fall over a fast-track for colocated load and generation that they said favored vertically integrated utilities. Same structure, different region, and they were on the other side of it.

Then there's the EIA number: more than 26 GW of battery storage next year, with renewables supplying almost a quarter of 2027 consumption while coal burn declines. A decade ago that battery figure would have been a fantasy; today it's a forecast buried in a summary.

And Edward Burgess's piece asks whether utilities will let data centers lower bills, since more sales spread over costs should push rates down. He says some are pairing old, lower sales data with new cost assumptions to lock rates high. Regulators in San Juan were just saying that customers want proof, and this is where proof gets decided: in the baseline year nobody reads.
