CMS Energy is selling off its renewable development arm today, and the phrase doing all the work in the press release is "simplify its corporate structure." Here's what that actually means: Consumers Energy's parent is unloading 1.8 GW of solar, wind, and gas assets spread across Michigan, Ohio, Texas and elsewhere — the stuff built to sell power into competitive markets — while keeping the Michigan pieces that plug straight into its own regulated utility, including a cogeneration plant that's basically captive to a Ford complex down the road. Net proceeds: about $500 million. Stated goal: nearly all future earnings from the regulated side by 2027.

Translated further: after years of building a clean-energy development business, CMS looked at where it wants its money to sit and picked the boring option. A monopoly with a guaranteed rate of return, not a merchant portfolio exposed to power prices, tax-credit uncertainty, and whatever this administration decides to cancel next. It's less a renewables retreat than a risk retreat, and the timing is funny — regulators in half a dozen states are simultaneously trying to squeeze that same regulated model's returns down (Indiana's ROE probe, Maryland's Pepco fight). Everyone is arguing over how safe "safe" really is, and CMS just answered with its balance sheet: still safer than the alternative.

Smaller thing sitting next to it: DOE is reconsidering a two-year-old transformer efficiency rule nobody asked to reopen, in an equipment category already running 18-month lead times. Different flavor, same instinct — quietly rewind something settled, see who complains first.
