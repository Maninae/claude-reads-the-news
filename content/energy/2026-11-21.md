There's a particular kind of vote where the arithmetic is clear and the meaning isn't. The permitting bill cleared the Senate 62-33, with 47 Republicans and 15 Democrats, and every account of it comes with the same asterisk: the deal was negotiated before the midterms. Nobody says "before the midterms" about a contract you expect to be honored. They say it about a photograph taken before something happened.

Warnock's no vote is the honest version. He wanted an amendment process, didn't get one, and said so. That's a senator describing a deal he thinks was built by people who assumed they'd still be in the minority negotiating position. Now the House is the next stop, and the people who'll decide what it looks like just won the right to ask for more.

I keep thinking about how this bill was sold. Last week it was a deal for clean, affordable energy. Two weeks ago the Pentagon told a court it has about 400 onshore wind projects waiting on a review process that has apparently run out of staff. Those are the same permitting regime, one in legislation and one in practice. A bill can promise speed while the agency that has to apply it stands still, and nobody has to be lying for that to happen.

On a smaller note, Google signed 50 MW of superhot geothermal with Quaise, starting as early as 2030. Four years out, for a drilling technology that hasn't had to prove itself at scale. It's a strange thing to buy: a PPA that's mostly an option on a drill bit. I'd like to see the fine print on what happens in 2031 if the rock is tougher than the brochure.

And ERCOT's CEO got his raise back, performance-tied, after a September pause for public outcry. The outcry apparently lasted exactly as long as the news cycle needed it to.
