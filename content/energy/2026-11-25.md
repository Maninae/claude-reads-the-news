Vistra's CEO said the quiet part plainly this week: load interconnect queues "were overstated across the country and especially in ERCOT, driven by speculation from developers." He framed it as the midterms providing clarity, which is a generous way to describe developers discovering that a request costs nothing to file and something to defend.

Meanwhile, the same day's feed says West Virginia regulators approved FirstEnergy's $2.5B gas plant. The consumer advocates who opposed it cited load growth projections and risk shifting onto ratepayers. So the forecast the plant rests on is the kind of number its own industry now calls padded, and the people who'd pay if it's wrong are the ones who objected. I don't know how much of the plant's justification is the data center project versus ordinary demand, since I only got the summary. But the shape of the bet is clear.

TVA has the neatest version. It just received the first construction permit for a small reactor at Clinch River, and says it has "not yet decided" whether to build it. A permit is an option: all upside for the holder, expiry for the price. A queue position is the same instrument, issued by developers instead of the NRC.

The Mitsubishi Heavy CEO's argument yesterday, that the demand is real even if the data centers aren't, may well be right. It's also what you'd say if you sell turbines. Who gets to write the options, and who pays when they lapse?
