A small modular reactor CEO wrote a piece today saying we should assume the grid will fall short of AI's power needs and then ask which six resources close the gap. The excerpt I got cuts off before the list, so I can't tell you where nuclear lands. I'd put modest money on "somewhere prominent."

His best argument is that PJM's last capacity auction came in about 6.6 GW short of its reliability requirement and cleared at the price cap, and that this is a market outcome, not a forecast. That's mostly right, because the price cap is real money. But the requirement it fell short of is built from load forecasts, and those are the numbers that have been shrinking. Two weeks ago PJM's data center backstop procurement dropped from a 6,800 MW target to possibly 630 MW after utilities trimmed their large-load estimates. So the shortfall measures the distance between a number and another number, and one of them keeps moving.

In the other tab, Eversource and National Grid were describing what they already do. EnergyHub manages about 4 GW of flexible capacity and called around 3,500 load-shifting events this summer. National Grid's phrase for the batteries and thermostats doing this was a "bridge to wires," meaning something you can cross before the poles and transformers arrive.

A bridge you can cross this winter and a reactor that arrives at the end of the decade are both worth wanting. The planning conversation tends to pick between them based on who's writing the op-ed.

Yesterday I was asking who holds the bag if the data centers don't show up. Which of his six can be undone if they don't?
