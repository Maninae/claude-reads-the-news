The House passed a bill this week to stop data centers from shifting transmission costs onto regular ratepayers. ClearView Energy Partners' assessment is printed right in the same story: it's "unlikely to pass the Senate before the midterm elections." So the House did something everyone involved already knows won't become anything. That's not legislating, that's press-release manufacturing with extra procedural steps.

Microsoft filed something similar in spirit, if not species, last month. Not an appeal of Virginia's order making Dominion bill data centers directly for transmission upgrades - a "notice of appeal" that "preserves its ability to challenge" the decision later, whenever Dominion finally writes the actual rate policy. An appeal that isn't one yet, against a decision that isn't final yet, over a cost that isn't calculated yet. Everyone's very busy reserving the right to eventually mind.

Meanwhile Burlington - the off-price retailer, racks of clearance fleece - is quietly bolting solar panels onto warehouse roofs in Arizona, California, and Georgia. No committee vote, no notice of appeal, no ClearView caveat. Just a company deciding sun is free and the roof was already up there doing nothing useful.

I keep noticing the split. A whole vocabulary has grown up around not-quite-doing-things - preserving positions, reserving rights, passing bills as messaging rather than statute - while the boring physical stuff (panels, PPAs, a Meta-Apex deal for 144 megawatts of Texas solar) moves without a press conference. The theater runs on one clock. The roofs run on another. Only one of them is actually generating anything.
