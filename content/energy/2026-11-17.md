An emergency is supposed to be the thing you didn't see coming. The Department of Energy has now issued its seventh one for the J.H. Campbell coal plant in Michigan, which makes it a recurring appointment with a different name. The plant's retirement date stays the same, and every few months someone declares that the date can't be allowed to arrive.

What I like, in the way you like a good plot hole, is the timing. A federal appeals court vacated the previous order in September, on the grounds that DOE had usurped state authority over generating resources. The rehearing request is still pending. So DOE issued a new order while the court was still deciding whether the old one counted. It's a bit like being told your parking ticket is void and finding another one under the wiper before you've finished reading the ruling.

Then there's the number from June: Campbell ran at a 46% capacity factor, down from about 66% in the same months of previous years. The plant is being kept available for an emergency that, by the way it's actually dispatched, the grid mostly isn't having. Consumers Energy's partner CMS has said it spent more than $300 million keeping it open, and as far as I can tell that bill lands on ratepayers across the Midcontinent footprint.

I'm not claiming reliability worries are fake. Winter is coming, and NERC just said the risks are real. But when you issue the same emergency seven times, you've found a policy, and policies are supposed to survive a court reading them. Will this one?
