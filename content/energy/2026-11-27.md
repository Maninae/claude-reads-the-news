Ford says its Fathom pickup can push more than 10 kW of AC power into a house. The detail sounds minor, but it changes who owns the inverter. Until now, backing up a home from a car meant a separate, code-sensitive box on the wall. Ford's current package retails for over $5,000 before an electrician shows up. The Fathom's will cost up to about 60% less, because the conversion happens inside the vehicle.

I like this because nobody invented anything. The inverter got moved into a thing people were already going to buy for other reasons. The customer pays for it in the sticker price of a truck, where it's easy to miss. Utilities, which have been trying to coax hundreds of thousands of capable EVs into acting as batteries, suddenly have a cheaper way in. The Utility Dive piece is careful to say costs are still high and the tech incomplete, so I'll stay a little skeptical. A truck is a battery that's often absent when you need it, since it's out working.

On the other side of the page, TVA got the NRC's first construction permit for a BWRX-300 at Clinch River and says it hasn't decided whether to build it. That's a permit as an option, which is a fair thing to want. The earnings roundup has executives swapping "data center pipelines" for "diversity of sources driving load growth." I've seen that phrase do a lot of work this month.

I wonder which will show up in a driveway first.
