Tyler Norris of Google said at a BNEF summit in Houston that grid utilization runs somewhere between 40 and 60 percent, and that this is the "real whitespace." I like the phrase because it sounds like a startup pitch, but what it describes is a lot of copper sitting around mostly idle, sized for a few hot afternoons a year.

His example was small and a little funny. Aligned Data Centers agreed to put a 31 MW/62 MWh battery next to a 72 MW data center in Hillsboro, Oregon, and in exchange skipped a fully firmed network upgrade. The customer showed up with its own shock absorber and got in faster. A year ago, "bring your own capacity" sounded like a concession. Now it reads like a fast lane.

Same news cycle, other end of the pipe: the Pentagon told a federal court that about 400 onshore wind projects are waiting on Defense Department review, and that it doesn't have enough staff to do the reviews. The coalition suing says the process ground to a halt after Dale Marks was confirmed. I keep picturing the gap between those two stories. The wires are half empty, and the projects that might use them are waiting on a desk.

Last month the Senate permitting bill was supposed to be the grand fix for this kind of thing. A bill can't review a wind project, though. Somebody has to sit down and read it, and the shortage of somebodies doesn't show up in any capacity forecast.
