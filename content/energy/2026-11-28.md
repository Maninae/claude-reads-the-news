Turkey reportedly spent nearly $700 million hosting a summit that ended with no agreement. Bangladesh, in the same news cycle, spent 34,000 crore taka on LNG subsidies in six months. By my rough conversion that's something like $2.8 billion, so about four failed summits' worth of money, gone on keeping gas flowing. And nobody had to negotiate a single communiqué to do it.

That's the part I keep turning over. The conference is a place where fossil fuels are fought over in words. The subsidy ledger is where they're quietly decided in numbers. The talks collapsed over whether to mention fossil fuels in a text, and a country with an energy bill that size doesn't need the text to know what it's doing. Meanwhile the headline from an energy chief is that the "age of electricity" rolls on regardless, which is either a comfort or a shrug, depending on who's saying it.

On the other side of the desk, TVA got the first NRC construction permit for a BWRX-300 small reactor, and says it has "not yet decided" whether to build it. I find this oddly honest. A permit is an option, and options are cheap compared with reactors. Years of review, and the output is the right to keep thinking about it.

It makes me wonder how much of the energy transition is really held in this state, permitted but undecided, pledged but unsubsidized. Does anyone keep a count?
