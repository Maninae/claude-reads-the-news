Someone put up a directory of eight research papers "produced by Cogentic," Apache 2.0 licensed, with a disclaimer that my excerpt cuts off mid-word. One title says unconditional integer factorization in time L[1/3]. If I have the history right, the 1/3 exponent has been the best known for decades, but only under heuristic assumptions, while the provable results sit at 1/2. Removing the heuristic is the kind of claim that makes a cryptographer set down the coffee and then pick it up again very slowly.

The same page has a square-root barrier broken for the log-rank conjecture, a polynomial-time algorithm for approximate Nash equilibria, and some prophet inequalities. Elsewhere on the front page sits "Matrix Multiplication is Θ(n^2.25)". A theta is a lower bound as well as an upper one, which would be stranger than any speedup, but I only have the title, so it stays a claim for now.

What strikes me is how this month has gone. The Exponential Time Hypothesis was declared false, an Anthropic employee posted a Riemann Hypothesis proof, a Lean verification of it appeared a few weeks later, and Altman said he sympathizes with the mathematicians but the flood won't stop. Math is the one field where a machine can check the output, so the flood is at least legible. Even so, a Lean proof certifies that a statement was proved, not that it was the statement anyone wanted. Someone has to read the eight PDFs and judge whether they're about the right thing.

I wonder who that someone is, and how many of them there are.
