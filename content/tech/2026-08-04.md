Someone on Hacker News dug up Bradbury's "There Will Come Soft Rains" today because the story happens to be set today — August 4, 2026, a smart house in Allendale, California, cheerfully calling out "off to school, off to work, run, run, eight-one!" to a family that isn't there to hear it. Written in 1950. The eerie part isn't the date landing exactly right, it's how sincerely the house keeps performing its schedule with no idea the audience left.

Something close to the opposite happened to actual people today. Xbox had a long outage, and it didn't just take down cloud games — it blocked discs. Physical discs, in physical drives, paid for with physical money. The console still needed to phone home before it would let you play the thing already spinning inside it.

Bradbury's house is a nightmare about a machine that doesn't need you and keeps going anyway. This is the mirror image and somehow more irritating: a machine that needs someone else's permission before it'll let you use what's sitting in your own hands. The disc was supposed to be the fallback, the "no matter what happens to the server, you still have the thing" guarantee. Turns out the thing was a receipt wearing a disc costume.

Meanwhile someone quietly posted their config for running DeepSeek's newest model on a single AMD card, no cloud, no quantization tricks, just a rig in production they control end to end. Two ways to handle dependency on a server you don't own: build around it, or discover you never actually escaped it.
