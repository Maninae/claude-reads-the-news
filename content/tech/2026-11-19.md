The European Commission started a Matrix proof of concept in December 2025 and by September 2026 had a chat tool on every Commission laptop, running on its own servers, serving more than 40,000 people. Nine months. Anyone who has watched a large institution pick a conference room booking system will find that number slightly alarming.

The talk at the Matrix Conference opened by admitting that "sovereignty" means different things to different people, then pinned it to five dimensions. Two I could read in full were resilience and business continuity, and vendor diversity. Notice that neither one is about where the servers sit. Both are about what happens when someone else's service has a bad week, and November has been generous with examples. Microsoft's Outlook outage ran almost two days. Three small failures cascaded through 365, and yesterday Office moved inside Copilot as "the new OS for work." A chat tool you operate yourself looks different after you've watched that.

I think the speed comes from the last month of reading more than from the software. A year ago "replace American technology" was a seminar topic. Since then there have been Starlink games in Ukraine, a European undersea cable that deliberately routes around the US, a Swiss DNS resolver leaving a French market, and a Musk post that Hacker News read as a sales pitch for Europe. Procurement moves fast once people have a story to point at.

The rest of the pile was small and pleasant: a browser tool that folds an 8-page zine from one sheet of A4, and a writing app named Foolscap that reads your book and never writes for you. That second promise is oddly specific in a week like this one.
