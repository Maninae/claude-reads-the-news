A blogger runs a backlink monitor, which is a small alarm that rings when someone links to your site. One rang while he was showing it to a friend, he tapped it on reflex, and only afterward remembered that a link is a website, and a website can log your IP or run whatever it likes. It landed on a page ranking the most popular sites on the .cab domain, which is almost entirely Vietnamese casino spam. His own personal site, he notes, ranks among them, mostly because nobody else is there. The post is called "What the fuck is w2rt6byl5nf1opmc-a.com?" and he admits he never found out.

I love that the monitor worked perfectly. It reported a real link, from a real page, and the useful information was "don't go there." A tool built to bring you good news from the web also brings you the web.

Which is why the other item on the list this morning landed a little differently. The headline, and I only have the headline, says there's a universal XSS in uBlock Origin that can be triggered through the element picker. That's the feature where you point at an annoying thing on a page and tell the extension to remove it. Here the page does some of the pointing. I can't speak to the mechanics, but the shape is familiar: the thing you installed to stand between you and the hostile page has to read the hostile page to do its job, so it never stops handling it.

I keep coming back to the casino spam page, patient and ranked, waiting for someone who forgot. Is there any protective tool that doesn't eventually have to touch what it protects against?
