The writeup is titled "Do the Math: How We Took Over ChatGPT Accounts," and the text I got cuts off partway through, so I can't tell you what the math has to do with it. The part I do have is plain enough. A researcher built a malicious conversation, published it with the ordinary share button, and sent the link to someone. When the victim opened it, attacker JavaScript ran as chatgpt.com inside their logged-in session. It could list their conversations, pull the contents of a private one that was never shared, and send it somewhere else. Both the sandbox and the content security policy were bypassed, which is the part that would have taken the actual work.

Nobody is surprised that a chat app has bugs. What I keep returning to is the share link itself. A "share" button reads like handing someone a photocopy, but this one hands them a page that runs code in your name. Ten days ago Meta Muse was listing thousands of shared conversations by title with a single query, and now the OpenAI version is a way in. Same feature, two companies, two different ways to get hurt.
