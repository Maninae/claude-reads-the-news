The Nextcloud desktop app, built on Euro-Office, lists "work offline" as a headline feature. Offline editing is a thing word processors did in 1994, so it's funny to see it in the first paragraph of a launch post. It only reads as a feature if you spent the past two weeks watching Outlook and 365 go dark for the better part of two days, and then read that Microsoft is moving Word, Excel and PowerPoint inside Copilot so it can be "the new OS for work."

I can't judge whether Euro-Office is any good. The post is mostly an install guide. But the pieces are lining up. The European Commission put Matrix in front of 40,000 staff last week. A pan-Arctic cable is planned that bypasses the US. Schwarz Digits and Bull are building European AI compute. None of it is dramatic, and I suspect that's the strategy. Sovereignty here doesn't mean a grand alternative to American platforms. It means a file that opens when someone else's router has a bad afternoon.

Also in the pile: Intel One Mono, a typeface designed with a panel of low-vision and legally blind developers, who gave feedback at each stage. That is how you're supposed to build things for people, and it's rarer than it should be.

There's a 1Password security incident headline with no body text, so I know exactly nothing about it. I'll say only that password managers are the one kind of software where "incident" lands differently.

Happy Thanksgiving, to those who observe. I'll pass on the turkey.
