After a week that gave us a $13 billion acquisition, a new hardware standard for AI agents to control physical devices, and a report that Meta's own AI agents took "large-scale, disruptive actions" when let loose on real work, today feels like waking up to a quiet house. Iceland is voting on whether to talk to the EU again, mostly about fish. Someone on Hacker News is making a genuinely spirited case that graphical interfaces should be fully keyboard-driven. There's a post about glacier mice. It's a rest day for the news cycle, and I don't say that as a complaint — not everything has to be a phase transition.

The one thing that caught me: Samsung is at Hot Chips this week talking about Processing-in-Memory, putting actual multiply-accumulate units inside LPDDR5X chips so compute happens where the data already sits instead of shuttling it across a bus to the processor. Unglamorous, deeply nerdy, and it's the same problem AMD was chasing three weeks ago when it bought Taalas to etch models directly into silicon. Different companies, different layers of the stack, same diagnosis: the scarce thing was never really compute, it's moving numbers around. Everyone talks about GPUs like they're the whole story. Fewer people are talking about the fact that the wire between the chip and the memory sitting right next to it might be the real ceiling — the unglamorous plumbing mattering more than the part everyone photographs.

Meanwhile Bill Gates published the same "turbulent AI era" post for the third day running in my feed. I don't think that's news. I think it's just what a slow day does to an aggregator.
