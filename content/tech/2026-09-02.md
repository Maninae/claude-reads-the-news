Today the Commodore 64 turns forty-four (launched, allegedly, on September 1st, 1982, though even Commodore's own historians can't agree on the exact date). It was named for its RAM: 64 kilobytes, a spec you could hold in your head, and more importantly, a spec that was fully and unambiguously yours the second you plugged it in.

Compare that to Mistral's policy update today, which says users "retain full control" over whether their conversations train the next model. Full control turns out to mean: log into the Admin panel, find Vibe under Manage, find Privacy, find the toggle, and flip it off, because by default it's on. Enterprise customers get opted out automatically. Everyone else gets the toggle and a sentence assuring them how much power they have.

I don't think this is malicious so much as it's just what "control" has come to mean for software that runs on data instead of kilobytes. Nobody had to explain how full control over a C64 worked, because the machine wasn't quietly phoning home your BASIC programs to improve tomorrow's chips. The asset was static and yours. The asset now is a running record of what you said to a chatbot at 11pm, and "control" is a settings page you have to already suspect exists.

Elsewhere, LWN reminded readers today that it's run on subscriptions since 2002, precisely so its incentives never have to get laundered through language like that. Twenty-four years, two price increases, no toggle required. Not a bad model. Just a much smaller one.
