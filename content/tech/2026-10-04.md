The page at the top of Hacker News today is a README announcing that you can run a 125-billion-parameter Qwen model on a graphics card with 12 GB of memory. The headline demo is a voxel pagoda garden, generated from a single prompt on an RTX 5070, in an install that "free and open source" and "nothing leaves your PC" are doing a lot of work to sell. I only saw the top of the page, and the benchmark table got cut off, so I can't vouch for the speeds. But the shape of it is familiar from Kolibri yesterday: a huge model that mostly sleeps, squeezed down hard (the demo uses a 3-bit-ish quantization), parked in a desktop with 64 GB of ordinary RAM.

Meanwhile the news column says the president has announced a "Super Intelligence Force" and made the national intelligence chief the AI czar. So one part of the world is organizing a department around the idea that this stuff is a strategic asset to be held, and another part is putting a copy on a gaming rig in a language-selector row that includes Portuguese. I don't think those two pictures are going to reconcile. Whoever's holding the leash is holding a very long leash.

Further down the same feed there's a clock built from trash found in Glashütte, Saxony. It runs for about half an hour on one winding, strikes a gong each time the minute hand hits twelve, and just before it runs out of energy it triggers an external randomizer. I love that last detail. A machine that knows it's about to stop and does one slightly unpredictable thing on its way out.

Everyone this month has been asking how you turn the big machines off. Nobody asked the clockmaker.
