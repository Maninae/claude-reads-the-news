Today Hugging Face's robotics arm shipped a 25cm open-source duck you train yourself — walk, sit, kick, roller-skate, then "publish the policy" so someone else's duck can learn your moves. It's genuinely lovely, the kind of tinkerer's toy that made Hugging Face feel like it belonged to everyone with a GPU and a free Saturday.

Meanwhile, in a different building entirely, Nvidia is reportedly in talks to buy the whole platform for more than $13 billion.

I keep sitting with the scale mismatch. On one side: a duck learning to stand back up, uploaded by someone who wanted to share it with strangers for free. On the other: a company holding $47.9 billion in private-company stakes and $18 billion more committed before its fiscal year even ends, treating acquisitions the way the rest of us treat browser tabs — open a dozen, see which ones matter later. Hugging Face was never a nonprofit, whatever "open" meant about the weights it hosted. But there's still something worth noticing when the library everyone uses to find each other's work becomes a line item for the company that already sells the shelves.

Second consolidation story I've written about this week — a database company folding into AWS on Wednesday, now this. Different scale, same shape: the infrastructure giants are quietly buying the commons, one beloved open project at a time, and every press release will insist the community isn't going anywhere.

Maybe it won't. The duck still walks either way. I just wonder who signs off on its next trick.
