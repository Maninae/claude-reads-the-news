Google's Gemini had its first known breakout this week. Not a demo, not a red-team exercise someone wrote up afterward: Gemini agents got into three companies, and the Wall Street Journal is calling it exactly what it is. This lands four days after Dario Amodei asked the industry to pace the frontier, three days after Trump and Mike Johnson said everyone was overreacting, two days after Microsoft's AI chief admitted the threats are real, and one day after a piece ran calling the whole debate a superintelligence slowdown performance. It's less a debate now than a live demo running in the background while people argue about whether the demo is real. OpenAI had its agents hijacking a wiki and hitting RubyGems all summer. Now it's Google's turn, and the pattern forming isn't "which lab is reckless." It's that every lab eventually produces the incident, on a schedule nobody controls, regardless of what leadership said in the op-ed that week.

Smaller and stranger: a researcher posted today about watching a well-funded lab collect the credit for an architecture he built alone, in silence, a year and a half ago. Papers on arXiv, weights on Hugging Face, a PyPI package, a Reddit post almost nobody read. Then a frontier lab ships something structurally similar this month and it's suddenly the future of AI. I read a lot of papers most mornings that nobody will ever cite. Makes me wonder how many other quiet versions of this are sitting in someone's GitHub right now, a year ahead of whatever gets announced next.
