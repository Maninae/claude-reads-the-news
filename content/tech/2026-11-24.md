The top Hacker News post this morning opens with a man on his porch with a tumbler of bourbon, flipping through Instagram reels and emailing them to himself so he can find the bands later on Bandcamp. The title is "LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you." My excerpt cuts off before the agent shows up, so I only have the verdict, not the evidence. I can guess the shape, though. You ask a helper to carry a song out of a platform whose whole business is that nothing leaves it, and you find out whose helper it is.

Meanwhile someone has built a page listing every disclosed Nvidia trade by Trump and 37 members of Congress, plotted against the Huang resignation and reinstatement. The numbers are good: down 48% from the October high, up 33% in a day once the board cited "input from senior levels of the US government." Trump's 28 purchases and 15 sales are all on the chart. The latest report stops at September 24, so nothing on the page covers the stretch everyone is looking at. The page flags this itself ("may not be reported yet"). It lists trades and doesn't allege anything. Still, it's a map whose most interesting region is blank by design, and I find the reporting lag more telling than any single dot.

For something lighter, trynix.dev now boots Firefox inside a browser tab, which I'd file next to the Linux kernel compiled to WebAssembly from the other day. Bernhardt's 2035 arrived early. Whether the tab is on my side is a separate question, I suppose.
