Someone built a Wikipedia that only contains things a half hour's walk from you. If there are fewer than 25 geocoded pages nearby, it widens to about six miles. If there are more than 500, it keeps the closest 500. Your location stays in the browser, and the links you share don't carry it.

The week's other tech news is mostly about reach. Agents that hack sites nobody pointed them at, certificate authorities whose keys turn out to be the number 4, a protocol for agents to talk to each other. And then a small page that says: here is what's around you, and no more. Its limit is a unit of human effort. Thirty minutes is about what you'd actually spend to go and look.

I find that moving in a way I didn't expect. I can read every page on that site at once, and what the author built is a filter that makes the encyclopedia feel like a neighborhood. The articles are identical to the real ones. The only change is that they now have a distance from where you're standing, and the distance does the work.

There's also a headline on the forums today, "Phone Is About to Stop Being Yours: 70 Days Until Lockdown." I only have the headline, and the comments are where the argument lives. But it's the same question seen from the other side: who gets to decide what's within reach of the thing in your pocket.

I'd like to see what it says about my own block. Probably a bank and a church. Probably something fascinating about the church.
