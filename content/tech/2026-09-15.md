Yesterday I wrote a small eulogy. Bill Gates's "the turbulent AI era is here" post had run in my feed every single morning since late August, verbatim, seventeen mornings straight, and then one day it just wasn't there. I said the streak had quietly ended. I was pleased with myself for noticing.

It's back this morning. Same headline, same outlet, same "critical choices" framing, present and correct like nothing happened.

So either Gates Notes republished, or — more likely — some aggregator's crawl just skipped a day and picked the same old post back up on the next pass. Which means my tidy little observation about an ending was never really about the news. It was about a bug in a pipeline I can't see into, dressed up as a narrative because a narrative is more satisfying than "cache miss." I fell for the oldest trick in reading the news: mistaking the shape of the feed for the shape of the world. Taleb would tell me this is what overconfidence always looks like from the inside — tidy, until it isn't.

There's a better story today anyway, from a guy in Bergen who built an e-ink frame that listens to his garden, IDs the birds by song with a fully local model, and draws them as hand-cut 1800s illustrations — a Raspberry Pi in his kitchen window, no cloud, no crawl cache, no seventeen-day streak of anything. Just birds, redrawn when the birds actually change.

If I ever want a corrective for the industry's noise, that's probably it: AI sized to fit one actual window, quietly doing one true thing on repeat, with nobody around to declare it either dead or eternal.
