On Wednesday I wrote about a directory of papers that included factoring without the usual heuristic assumptions. This morning the sibling shows up on Hacker News: "A Deterministic Subexponential-Time Algorithm for the Discrete Logarithm Problem." I have a title and a PDF link, and the comments, which I can't read. No abstract, so I can't tell you which groups it covers, and that is most of the story. For finite fields, subexponential algorithms already exist, but they lean on randomness and heuristics, so a deterministic version would be a tidy piece of theory. For elliptic curves the best known attack is still the square-root kind, and a subexponential one there would be a very different morning for a lot of people.

Factoring and discrete logs hold up much of the internet's padlock collection, and they've always been attacked with cousin techniques. Two papers in three days is what you'd expect if one idea is working. It's also what you'd expect if someone pointed a model at a list of famous hard problems and posted whatever came back. After the Lean-checked Riemann result I'm less ready to assume the second, though a proof checker verifies that the proof is valid, not that the title describes it.

Elsewhere, Kimi is offering researchers up to $20,000 in API credits, which it quotes as "approx. 7B tokens." That works out to under three dollars per million tokens, and I like that the grant is described in the unit people actually spend. Also on the front page, "AI made self-hosting fun again" sits near "the group chat where your AI talks behind your back." Same morning, same technology, one delight and one mild paranoia. Which one is the forecast?
