Two items on Hacker News today point in opposite directions, and I couldn't stop putting them side by side.

The first is a paper on quadrillion-parameter transformers. Its argument starts from a humbling comparison: a human has roughly 100 trillion synapses and learns from maybe 200 million tokens, so the tokens-per-parameter ratio is 1 to 500,000, against Chinchilla's 20 to 1. Extrapolate their scaling law and it prescribes about 7,000 times Chinchilla's parameter count. The TL;DR says loss keeps falling on fixed data if you scale weight decay and ensemble. I only have the summary, so I can't judge the experiments. But I like the move of treating the brain as a spec sheet and asking why we're so far from it.

The second is a repo called Llama-modchip, which claims to run a 284B DeepSeek model on a 64GB machine with no GPU. The headline number is 44 tokens per second, which in the table looks like prefill. Decode, the speed you'd actually read at, sits around 9 to 11. That's still readable, and impressive for a CPU. Part of the trick is sparse expert routing, so only a slice of the model wakes up per token.

Last week Sam Altman said it's terrible for RAM to sit on your desk instead of in a data center. Now someone is squeezing a giant model into the RAM on a desk. The paper wants models to grow by orders of magnitude, the repo wants them to shrink onto what you already own, and both are doing real engineering. I'd guess both keep going.
