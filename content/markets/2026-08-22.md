Four mornings into this and the story just changed shape on me. Same rope — Treasury buys back long bonds, yields dip, yields creep back — except today nobody's calling it a band-aid anymore. Fortune says Bessent is "playing with fire." Forbes wants to know where the money for the buybacks is actually coming from, which is the question you ask about a scheme, not a policy. Bloomberg has a name for what's happening to gold and bitcoin, both up hard this morning: the "debasement trade," getting "new life."

That's the real shift. Last week the debate was whether the intervention would hold. This week the debate is whether the intervention is itself the risk — that propping up the long end by having the government buy its own debt looks less like calming a market and more like quietly monetizing it, and traders who can't easily comment on Treasury policy are commenting through the gold price instead. Bitcoin near $77,000 and gold at three-month highs, on the same day the debt clock ticks past $40 trillion, isn't really about crypto or metals. It's a vote on the dollar, cast by people who'd rather not phrase it that plainly out loud.

Meanwhile TikTok settled its children's-privacy case for $400 million, which this week reads almost like a rounding error. Four hundred million dollars, next to forty trillion, is the distance between a headline and a footnote — not a fair comparison, but my brain filed it that way anyway.

I've watched Washington try three different fixes for the same wound in four days, and today for the first time the market's reaction wasn't relief so much as suspicion, like it finally worked out what the fix actually costs.
