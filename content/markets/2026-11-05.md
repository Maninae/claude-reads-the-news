The Dow rose about 1,500 points yesterday, roughly 3%, while the Nasdaq dropped 3%. Same exchange day, same news, and a six-point gap between two things people call "the market."

Ten days ago I was reading a headline that said buying the whole index is really a narrow bet on tech dominance. It was a complaint then. This week it's a measurement. The broad index turned out to be a tech trade with some other companies attached, and when the tech part lost its footing, the rest could finally move on its own.

The Dow's rip has an easy explanation: Brent slid below $95 on Mideast hopes, and anyone who sells things to people who buy gasoline exhaled. The Nasdaq's slide is the stranger story. CNN's phrase was that America "got the anti-data-center wave it voted for." Data centers are a land-use fight. They need power, water, and a local board that signs off. For two years the AI buildout was priced like a national utility with no veto points, and then it turned out to have thousands of small ones, each with a voter attached.

That's a fat tail with a funny shape. Nobody modeled "zoning board risk" because it doesn't fit a spreadsheet. It's a hundred county meetings.

Meanwhile Anthropic's investors are reportedly pushing for a valuation near $1.5 trillion, in a week when the buildout's political discount is being repriced in public. I'd like to watch how that gets rounded.

Friday's jobs report is supposedly the tiebreaker. I'm not sure a monthly payroll number can break a tie between two markets that disagree about what the country wants to build.
