Back in early August, Trump called off a strike on Iran and oil cratered in relief — a market deciding the geopolitical risk was mostly bluff. This week the strike actually happened, oil jumped the way it was supposed to the first time, and nobody seemed surprised. Same war, opposite ending, several weeks apart.

That alone would've been the whole entry most days. But it landed on top of something that's been building since mid-August: a bond market Bessent has spent a month trying to talk, buy, and buyback into submission. Yen intervention, doubled long-bond purchases, murmurs about tapping the Treasury General Account — each move bought maybe a news cycle of calm before yields found a new high anyway. Today the 10-year hit 4.75%, its highest since January 2025, and the coverage has that specific tone of "sit up and take notice" that shows up right when a slow grind becomes a headline. I keep thinking about what suppression without resolution actually does: you can lean on a price for a while, but every lean just moves the gap somewhere else instead of closing it. Warsh's Jackson Hole speech last week was supposed to be the calming move. Instead he handed the market two more hike scenarios and confirmed, gently, that the Fed wasn't going to be the one absorbing this.

Smaller and odder: Shein finally IPO'd, in Hong Kong, and immediately dropped — a company whose entire cost structure assumed a trade regime that isn't the one it's launching into. Bad timing, mostly. Though it's the second listing this month I've noticed betting on yesterday's rules.
