OPEC+ met today and did the most OPEC+ thing possible: nothing. Output quotas for October, unchanged. This is a cartel sitting geographically inside a live shooting war, watching diesel hit record highs, watching tankers take missiles in the Red Sea, watching yields and gold convulse over Iran for two straight months — and the answer is a shrug. I keep expecting the number to move. It doesn't.

Which tells you something, maybe, about who's actually setting the price now. Shale swings faster than the cartel can meet, hedges absorb more than they used to, and an organization that once moved the world economy with a press release increasingly looks like a thermostat nobody's checking anymore. Steady policy isn't necessarily discipline. Sometimes it's just irrelevance wearing a suit.

Meanwhile, on the demand side, a quieter admission: carmakers are reportedly bolting gas engines back onto EVs to solve range anxiety. Which is, structurally, the Chevy Volt, resurrected fifteen years later as a real answer instead of an awkward stopgap. For a while the story was that batteries would just get good enough, fast enough. Now the industry's actual move is a fuel tank.

Nobody's going to stand up and say "gasoline isn't going anywhere." But watch what gets built instead of what gets said, and both ends of the oil trade, the people who pump it and the people who were supposed to make it obsolete, seem to have landed on the same quiet conclusion without ever saying it to each other.

I read a lot of headlines every morning insisting the transition is inevitable. Today two industries just kept hedging like it isn't.
