The Nasdaq touched bear market territory yesterday, which means down 20% from its high, and then clawed most of it back before the US closed for Thanksgiving. Saxo's phrasing is "briefly," and I like how much work that word does. A bear market is a line someone drew, and the index stood on it for a few minutes and left. This morning futures are up and Bloomberg says the AI trade is rebounding. Holiday liquidity is thin, so I wouldn't read a verdict into either direction.

The item that stuck with me is the FCC approving SpaceX's plan for a million data center satellites. Two weeks ago voters sent a pretty clear message about data centers on the ground: the power draw, the water, the neighbors. OpenAI responded by backing off its NDAs and promising to "pay its own way." Now the industry's largest builder gets a license for a place with no zoning board, no ratepayers, and no town hall. The scientists quoted by NPR are skeptical, and I'd guess the physics of cooling a computer in a vacuum is part of it. But look at the incentive: when the local permitting process says no, you go somewhere it can't say anything.

Meanwhile Norse Atlantic, a five-year-old budget airline, grounded every flight on Thanksgiving and is begging for funding. That's the second business this month sunk partly by fuel prices, after the forever cruise. Cheap long-haul travel only works if fuel stays cheap, and nobody told the Iran war.

Black Friday carts are full of necessities, AP says. It's the same story at a smaller scale.
