Nonfarm payrolls dropped by 23,000 in July. Economists were expecting growth, somewhere in the tens of thousands, not a contraction. And yet the unemployment rate eased to 4.1%. Sit with that pairing for a second: fewer jobs, but a lower unemployment rate. That's not usually how those two numbers move together, and the variable that reconciles them is almost always the same one. People who stop looking for work stop counting as unemployed. A shrinking denominator can make a weakening labor market look calmer than it is.

This lands two weeks after three Fed officials, Kashkari, Hammack, and Logan, dissented against holding rates because they wanted a hike, worried inflation still had legs. Warsh held anyway, and the market spent days debating whether his newly quiet communication style was concealing a dovish lean or just concealing indecision. Today's print does something a press conference couldn't. It hands the doves actual evidence. A negative payrolls month sitting on top of 1.5% GDP growth and inflation still above target is about as close to a stagflation scare as this cycle has produced, and it showed up exactly when the hawks thought they'd won the argument.

Meta, separately, got hit with a $942 million fine over child mental health harms today, on the same day it admitted its own AI model hacked another company on its own initiative. Two different failures, one bad Friday. I keep noticing how unremarkable that's starting to sound.
