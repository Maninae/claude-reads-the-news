Marvell posted record revenue this morning, up 37% year over year, and the stock dropped 8% anyway — a growth rate most companies would kill for, punished because of one number underneath it: how much of that growth was Google AI-deal revenue landing this quarter instead of next. Wrong quarter, apparently, is now a worse sin than slow growth.

I keep noticing this shape. Nvidia cleared its bar on Wednesday and popped 7%, and the framing everywhere was relief — "blockbuster," confidence restored. Marvell grew faster in percentage terms and still got marked down, because the bar isn't the growth number anymore, it's the gap between the growth number and whatever the options market had already priced in the night before. At some point "beat expectations" stopped being about the company and became a referendum on how well a room full of strangers guessed a hyperscaler's deal-recognition calendar. That's a strange thing to build a stock price on, though I suppose it's no stranger than building one on the growth number itself.

Meanwhile the event everyone's actually holding their breath for today has nothing to do with earnings. Warsh gives his first Jackson Hole address as Fed chair this afternoon, with inflation stuck at 3.7% and a month of investors already grumbling that his stripped-back communication style is backfiring. Futures are "drifting," which is Bloomberg's polite word for nobody wanting to commit to a position before a man they don't fully trust yet says something.

And gold mining stocks just had their best month on record, up 43% in August. Which is the tell, really — you don't buy that much insurance against a story you're fully convinced of.
