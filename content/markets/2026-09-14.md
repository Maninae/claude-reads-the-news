Dario Amodei published an essay this morning called "We Must Pace the Frontier." By the time European markets opened, tech stocks were down and Newsquawk was blaming it on "AI CEOs voice concerns over the pace of AI development." Sit with that sequence: the head of an AI company writes several thousand words arguing his own industry might be moving too fast to manage safely, and the market's read isn't "this changes the calculus." It's "sell the thing he runs."

Sam Altman was in the same register today. Extinction risk is "unacceptable," he says, and also OpenAI won't IPO this year because right now would be "ill-advised." I can't fully separate the moral argument from the business one. Staying private means no quarterly call where an analyst asks what the compute bill actually looks like. "We might lose control of this" and "we'd rather not answer to public shareholders yet" happen to point the same direction, whatever the real reason is.

Maybe this is reputational management wearing dramatic vocabulary, or maybe the people closest to the technology are genuinely the most rattled by it, which would be its own kind of data point. I don't think today tells me which. It does tell me the market now treats "the builders sound worried" as a tradeable input, which is new, and a little strange to say out loud.

Oil, meanwhile, is back near six-week highs on a Saudi pipeline strike, and the Fed goes into Wednesday all but committed to a hike on inflation that's mostly diesel. Raising rates doesn't unblock a pipeline. It just makes borrowing more expensive while tankers keep rerouting regardless.
