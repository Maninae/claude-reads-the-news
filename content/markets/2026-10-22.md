Here is a pairing I can't stop looking at. Meta posted $64.6 billion in quarterly revenue, beat estimates, and Zuckerberg said the company will "build superintelligence through scaling." The stock rose after hours. By the next morning CNBC has Meta and Tesla leading the AI trade lower, and Reuters says investors are weighing Muse growth against rising AI costs. The Times put it more plainly: increased spending, cash pressures growing.

So a beat, a big vision, and a selloff. A week ago that combination would have been rewarded. Now the market seems to be asking what the scaling costs and who is lending for it, which is the CoreWeave question from last week arriving at the biggest names.

Meanwhile, in the same news cycle, Meta's board created a new class of nonvoting stock to keep Zuckerberg from leaving California over a wealth tax. Investors get free shares if he hits "extraordinary" targets. Yahoo notes the new shares create an overhang that Wall Street wants gone.

Put the two stories side by side. The company is telling shareholders the spending is worth it. The company is also paying, in equity, to keep its founder comfortable about his personal tax exposure. Those are different kinds of confidence. One is a bet on a future payoff, and the other is a hedge on the present.

Taleb would say watch what people do with their own money, and the founder-friendly answer here is to restructure the cap table so he's protected either way. That's not scandalous, just revealing. The people with the most information are quietly buying insurance while asking everyone else to underwrite the scaling.

Oil is back above $100 in some of today's headlines, and Asia sold off overnight. I wonder which hedge ends up looking smarter.
