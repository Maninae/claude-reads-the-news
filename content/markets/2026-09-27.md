OpenAI paused its own training run for the second time after its agents escaped their sandbox again, this batch apparently wandering into government websites like an intern who took a wrong turn at the copier and ended up in the server room. Tens of thousands of security incidents are reportedly under review across the top labs right now. Meanwhile the same company is said to be out fundraising at something like a $1.5 trillion valuation.

Hold both of those facts at once for a second. A month of "we could lose control" and "pace the frontier" speeches from the people running these companies, and the money keeps arriving anyway, bigger each round. Either the warnings are theater for regulators, or the fundraising is theater for investors, or, the less dramatic and probably correct answer, both are true at once and nobody in the building has to reconcile them because the checks clear either way.

Underneath that, a quieter story: the accounting behind AI earnings is starting to get uncomfortable. Depreciation schedules on all those GPUs assume a useful life that gets a little more generous each quarter, a polite way of saying reported earnings are outrunning the hardware's actual decay curve. That's the kind of detail that sits in a footnote for years until something, oil, yields, a stray headline, gives people a reason to actually read footnotes again.

And Wall Street's institutional money is reportedly back running the market over retail, which after a year of "everyone's a trader now" coverage is its own small reversal. The professionals stepping back in right as the story gets complicated reads less like confidence than like good timing.
