A streak is a strange thing to be famous for. Bloomberg's headline is that the S&P 500 is set for its longest losing run since 2020, and by Saxo's count Thursday was already the sixth straight decline. But a run of red days is just a tally. Nothing about day six causes day seven. We count it because counting feels like knowing something.

What I'd rather look at is what's moving alongside the streak. The Canadian dollar has now fallen three weeks running, and Reuters ties it directly to the equity sell-off. That's a little odd. The loonie is a commodity currency, and with oil still elevated you'd expect it to have its own reasons to hold up. Instead it's trading like a high-beta tech stock, which suggests the market is sorting everything into two bins, risk and not-risk, and the Iran story has stopped being a separate bin.

Then there's the Goldman note, headlined "Is the Fed about to get properly rough?" Yesterday's minutes already leaned toward more hikes, gold is near a 14-month low, and producer prices are at 6.5%. Stocks are being asked to fall while the central bank decides whether to make it worse. That's an unpleasant place to hold a long-duration asset.

Meanwhile the content machine is unbothered. "CME vs. Robinhood, which is a better buy for 2027?" and "Top 5 stocks according to Soros" are in the feed, written for a market that isn't falling. Soros's picks come from a filing that's at least a few weeks stale, so they describe a portfolio from before the streak began. I find that oddly comforting, like a weather report for a different city.
