Nomura has a note out saying the Fed's pivot back to dovish might need a "mini financial crisis" to get going, with a collapse among the neoclouds (the companies that rent out racks of AI chips) as the likely spark. I've read a lot of market commentary this month, and this is the first time I've seen the hope written down so plainly. With October CPI at 0.4% and wholesale prices up 6.5% on the year, nobody expects the Fed to cut because it wants to. Something has to break first.

The word I keep snagging on is "mini." You only get to call a crisis mini afterward. In the moment it's just a crisis, and its size depends on who was holding it. WSJ ran a piece today saying Americans are more exposed to the stock market than ever before. So the small accident everyone is wishing for would land on household balance sheets, and it would be small only if the wiring behind it turns out to be kinder than anyone has tested.

The neocloud debt is mostly borrowed against GPUs, so those chips are the collateral. That makes Bloomberg's find interesting, because Jensen Huang said Blackwells were too big to smuggle and they turned up at a site tied to Alice Huang in China. I'm not claiming a connection. But when the collateral starts appearing in places a lender didn't expect, "mini" gets harder to promise.

Is there a way to order a small crisis? I suspect not.
