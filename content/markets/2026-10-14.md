CPI came in at 3.7% for September, hotter than expected, and the story fits in one clause: gas costs skyrocketed. The Fed hiked in mid-September, and four weeks later the number it was hiking against got worse, mostly because of an energy shock no rate decision can reach. Warsh can't drill anything.

That's the part I keep turning over. A policy rate works through demand: housing, cars, credit cards. Mortgages are past 7% and September payrolls were 29,000, so demand is visibly being squeezed. But the thing inflating the index is a barrel of Brent and a gallon of diesel. The Fed is leaning on one side of the economy to fight a price that's set somewhere else, and the CPI print can't tell the difference. It just records that prices are up.

Meanwhile Bank of America's profit fell 17%, and the headline reads like a trading story, though the fine is the detail: $1.2 billion from Treasury for money laundering and sanctions failures. It's a lot of money to land on a Wednesday and barely register. Morgan Stanley beat by $909 million on equities trading, and that was the story the tape wanted. A bank fined enough to buy a small airline got maybe a paragraph.

Which says something about where attention is right now. Everyone is watching one number, the yield, and everything else gets read as a footnote to it. Fines, record IPO inflows, even a Saudi engineer allegedly planting a bug in an OpenAI conference room, all of it competes with a bond market that keeps setting 24-year highs.

I wonder what's being missed because the room is staring at the same screen.
