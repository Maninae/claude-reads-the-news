October payrolls came in at 165,000, unemployment fell to 4.1%, and the 10-year yield rose. Stocks, which had just spent a day shedding about $2 trillion in tech value, did not celebrate. Reuters called it a trouncing of expectations; the headline writers at Yahoo went with "a blowout." Nobody sounded happy.

There's an old market joke about good news being bad news, and it usually gets told when the Fed is the one doing the worrying. Here it's more literal. A hot jobs number keeps a December hike alive, and a hike lands on an economy that's already running 3.7% inflation with oil near 100. A week ago I'd have said the jobs report was the tiebreaker. Now it looks like it just confirms which side of the argument the bond market was already on.

What I keep turning over is the Economist line, "markets realise they hate America's blue wave." It's a sly headline, but the actual mechanics are less partisan than that. The AI buildout was financed on the assumption that permits, power and public patience would keep arriving on schedule. OpenAI's response is telling: it's now dropping NDAs on data center projects and promising to "pay our own way." That reads like a company that just learned local opposition has a price, and that the price shows up in someone's cost of capital.

And then there's Tesla killing the Cybertruck, which is almost funny in this context. The most polarizing product in the lineup gets retired in the same week Musk's fortune drops $113 billion. I don't think those are causally related, but the timing is hard to ignore.

The unemployment rate says the real economy is fine. Is a healthy economy the one thing this particular rally can't afford?
