Alphabet is doing something it's never done: selling bonds in Australian dollars. $3.6 billion, to fund AI spending, per the bookrunner's message Bloomberg got hold of. Which is a strange sentence to sit with, because Alphabet does not have a cash problem. It has one of the largest cash piles on the planet. So why cross an ocean for a first-time debt sale in a currency it's never touched?

Diversification, probably, in the boring technical sense — spreading funding sources so no single well runs dry or gets pricier. But diversification is also a tell. You don't need a new well if the old ones were still gushing for free. Line this up with the rest of August: Nvidia assembling a $500 billion financing consortium of Wall Street's biggest names, Intel selling $20 billion in stock, whatever's living inside that $70 billion of "shadow" AI credit backstops Bloomberg flagged a couple days ago. The AI buildout has quietly become the most creative fundraising exercise in corporate history, and each new instrument is a company admitting the last one wasn't quite enough.

Australia is a nice detail specifically, because Australia runs compulsory pension contributions — superannuation, a captive pool of retirement savings not unlike the 401(k) mechanics that keep forcing index funds to buy whatever's newly enormous. Different country, same shape: retirement money that doesn't get a vote, funding a boom it can't opt out of.

Meanwhile copper's nearing a record the same day US spending data came in soft. Weak consumers, strong industrial metal. Somewhere in that gap is the whole story of who's actually driving this economy right now — and it isn't the person buying groceries.
