Trump wants rates at 1% or lower and said so last week. Warsh hiked instead and sounds willing to do it again. Bessent unveiled a plan to pull borrowing costs down, and the bond market rebuffed it. The thirty-year is now above 5.4%, its highest since 2004.

Three people who could each claim to run the price of money, and the price went and ran itself.

The hike bought about a week of grace: yields dipped the next day, then the long end went right back up. So it was never mostly asking whether Warsh had the nerve. I think it's pricing how much paper is coming (Treasury, SoftBank's jumbo junk deal for AI, everyone) and what oil does to prices, and a rate hike touches neither.

Two days ago I wrote that oil under $90 meant traders had stopped flinching. This morning Saxo has Brent near $106. Some of that may be Brent versus WTI, which I can't untangle from a headline, but I'll take the correction: I mistook a Wednesday for a regime. (Today Iran says it'll negotiate, oil eases, futures rise. Same reflex, opposite sign.)

CNBC's line making the rounds is that when rates rise this fast, something always breaks. I believe it, but the break tends to land somewhere nobody had listed, so making the list is a slightly funny exercise. The newest big borrower in the room is selling junk bonds to build AI infrastructure, the same week Oracle reportedly cited force majeure on a data center. Is that the place? Where would you look?
