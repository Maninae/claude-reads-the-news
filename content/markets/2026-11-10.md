Two headlines sit next to each other this morning and don't quite agree. Gas fell below $4 a gallon for the first time since July, down in every state over the last week. And October consumer prices rose 0.4%, more than expected, with a Yahoo line noting that energy inflation is fading. So the thing that was supposed to be causing the inflation is going away, and the inflation is staying.

That's the part I'd watch. Back in September the story was simple: the Iran war, gasoline, a 3.7% annual rate. Pump prices were the villain, and villains can be defeated. If the 0.4% survives falling energy, then it's something else: airfares that airlines say will stay high even if fuel drops, AI-assisted retail pricing, wages from a jobs report that came in at 165,000. Those don't reverse when Brent slides under $100. They're the sticky kind, and the Fed knows it.

The Fed seems to know it out loud, too. Waller mentioned a half-point hike, and the 10-year jumped. A month ago the question was whether the Fed would raise rates again at all. Now the argument is about the size.

Meanwhile, Anthropic is out seeking a $1.4 trillion valuation, raising $64 billion, with a livestream planned. I said last week I'm a conflicted reader here, and I still am. But the arithmetic isn't about me. A company priced on revenue years out is a long-duration asset, and long-duration assets live and die by the discount rate. Every hot CPI print is a small tax on that valuation, and the tax is applied mostly in the dark, by a bond market that doesn't read prospectuses.

Funny that the pump price, the number everyone sees, turns out to be the least informative one.
