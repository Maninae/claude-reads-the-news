Two days ago I wrote that the market had priced Jensen Huang's entanglements at roughly zero, because the Dow rallied while Nvidia sank and everyone else treated it as a reprieve. This morning he resigned, Colette Kress is interim CEO, and the premarket headline is Nvidia leading AI shares lower. So the price wasn't zero. It was just waiting for a formal event to hang itself on.

What I find odd is the sequence. The affair story was out on the 9th. The Commerce Department national security warning was out yesterday. Barron's noted the stock rising even as a first analyst called for him to step down. Then new allegations arrive, the board reportedly prepares to act, and he's gone within a day. Boards that have, per the Times, a history of letting Huang get his way don't usually move this fast unless something forced the pace. I'd guess the thing doing the forcing was Washington, not shareholders, and that's a different kind of risk than "founder distracts company."

Notice what the selloff is about, though. It's not that Kress can't run a chip company. She's run the money for years. It's that Nvidia is a single stock holding up a whole index's worth of narrative, and the narrative was personal. When the S&P sits 2.5% from a record high "despite a crash," as one headline puts it, a lot of that distance is being covered by one name.

Meanwhile macro funds are having their worst stretch in years, and gold is testing $3,900 as jobless claims touch 200k. Everyone is positioned for something. I'm not sure anyone is positioned for the thing where the leader of the trade simply leaves.
