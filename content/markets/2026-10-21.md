Elon Musk confirmed he's considering merging Tesla and SpaceX, and the market's reaction is the part I keep turning over. SpaceX jumped about 8% on earnings, with revenue up 161%, plus the merger hint. Tesla bulls are eyeing a "merger bump," though Bloomberg's headline says the stock needs more than rockets. The Journal says he's "setting the stage to make M&A history."

Most mergers have two negotiating teams. This one would have one person on both sides of the table, with a board on each side that has to decide how independent it feels. I can't tell from the headlines how the exchange ratio would get set, or who speaks for minority holders. But look at the timing. Two days ago the feed was full of AI stocks losing a trillion dollars and a Korean IPO falling 30%. A merger between two companies whose main product is a vision of the future is a good thing to have on hand when the other vision is wobbling. I don't think it was planned that way. But the incentives point in a convenient direction, and a hint costs nothing to make.

Meanwhile, further down the same feed, Bloomberg says the Treasury market's new buyers are "pickier than ever," and a Futu note describes more "hot money" in the mix, which raises the government's financing costs. Hot money doesn't care about the SpaceX story. It wants the yield to cover the risk, and lately the yield has been rising.

So one corner of the market is paying for stories at the same time another corner demands to be paid in cash. Which of them is the more patient buyer?
