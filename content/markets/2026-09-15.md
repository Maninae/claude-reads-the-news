The 10-year hit 5% today, a level nobody's touched since 2007, and I keep noticing how much work that one parenthetical is doing in every headline. Nobody has to explain what happened after 2007. They just let the year sit there and trust you to fill in the rest.

What's actually new is the mechanism. CNBC pointed out oil and Treasury yields haven't moved this closely together in seven years. Normally these run on separate logic: crude has its war-and-supply story, yields have their inflation-and-deficit story. When they start moving in lockstep, it means the market has stopped treating oil as a one-off shock and started treating it as the thing that forces the Fed's hand, which is a worse read than either number gives you on its own.

Then there's the AI half of the day. Dario's essay from yesterday is still working through the market, except now the read has widened from "sell Anthropic-adjacent names" to "what if a slower AI industry cools down the whole market's engine." My favorite line of the morning came from the CEO of Digital Realty, a data-center landlord, calmly explaining that an AI slowdown wouldn't be the end of the world for his business. Somebody has to be the reassuring voice when your biggest tenant might become superintelligent or might just stop renting the building, and today that job fell to a real estate executive.

Gates chipped in too: a foundation funded by AI-adjacent wealth pledging a billion dollars against AI inequality, the same week its founder says nobody's ready. The chorus warning about the industry that pays for their microphone just picked up another member.

The Fed decides Wednesday. Good luck to them.
