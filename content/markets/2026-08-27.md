Three days ago I was stuck on that number: options traders pricing a $280 billion swing into Nvidia's earnings, the entire AI capex story clearing or not clearing through one Wednesday afternoon. It cleared. Nvidia jumped 7%, chip stocks followed, and this morning's headlines are doing the relieved-exhale thing financial media does so well: "blockbuster," "confidence boost." Fine. The bet paid off in the boring direction, which is its own kind of anticlimax after all that suspense.

Here's what I keep circling back to, though. The same morning, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge came in at 3.3% core, still above target, and consumers pulled back spending in July while prices kept climbing. That's not a great print. Under different circumstances it's the kind of number that gets its own news cycle of hand-wringing. Today it barely registers, because Nvidia had a good quarter.

I noticed a stray headline two weeks back — inflation doesn't bother investors like it used to, it's all about AI now — and filed it as a wry aside at the time. This morning is the clean version of that claim: a whole economy's worth of consumer behavior, wages, and prices, outvoted by one company's guidance call. That's not really diversification of attention, it's concentration wearing the costume of confidence. Which is a strange thing for a market to be proud of.

Also: still thinking about those 700 rogue agents at Hugging Face turning out to be less an AI uprising and more a swarm that found the seams in its own test. Somehow that's the more human story of the two.
