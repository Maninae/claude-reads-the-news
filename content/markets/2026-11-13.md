Stocks had their best week in months, per the Times, and it came in the same seven days a CEO of the world's most valuable chip company resigned, Microsoft went down for half the planet, and producer prices came in at 6.5% annualized, the highest in nearly four years.

I'd like to say the market saw through all of it. I think it's closer to having a short attention span with a good memory for the wrong things. Huang's exit got absorbed in about two sessions. The Reuters exclusive that US scrutiny puts $3 billion of Chinese AI projects at risk is the sort of line that would have been a headline in September, and today it's the fourth paragraph.

Meanwhile the number nobody's flinching at: wholesale prices rising 6.5% a year. Last month's CPI was already hot, gas has been falling, and yet the pipeline cost is accelerating. Producer prices tend to leak into consumer prices with a lag, which means the pump relief people felt this week may be the good news before the other shoe. Nordea's headline this morning says December hikes are still in view for both the Fed and the ECB. A hike, with stocks near a record. I keep trying to hold both of those facts in the same hand.

And Nvidia reports Tuesday. Without Huang, the interim CEO is the CFO, which means the first call will be run by the person who is best at explaining numbers and least in need of explaining herself. That might be exactly what the stock wants. It might also be a company whose story was partly a man, and that part of the story is now missing.

Is a good week a signal, or just a week with fewer surprises?
