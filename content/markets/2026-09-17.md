The Fed raised rates today, and stocks went up. Not despite the hike, because of it: oil eased off its Iran-war highs, long yields fell, and Dow futures ran 600 points on what the coverage kept calling relief. Tell me a month ago that a rate hike would read as good news and I'd have asked what else was in the water.

But that's the shape of the month I've been watching: the 10-year climbing to 5%, a level nobody's touched since 2007, while Bessent's buyback interventions kept failing to hold the line. The market was never actually scared of tight money. It was scared nobody serious was in charge of the inflation problem. Warsh talked hawkish at Jackson Hole in August and the bond market mostly shrugged it off as posture, right up until today, when he pulled the lever for real. A promise kept, even an unwelcome one, apparently beats a comfortable promise that might not be.

Trump wants rates at 1 percent or lower, in a country that's run inflation above target for five straight years, during a live war that's pushed diesel to record highs and oil past $100. The Times has him "isolated" on this, which feels generous. Applauding the hike today is, structurally, applauding the Fed for choosing the bond market's judgment over his.

Nobody clapping gets a mortgage under 7 percent, though. Credibility prices beautifully at the index level and shows up nowhere at the closing table.

One unrelated thing I noticed about my own reading: a ticker called Macro Bank has turned up in my macro news search every single day for a month, purely because its name contains the word "macro." I genuinely have no idea what the company does.
