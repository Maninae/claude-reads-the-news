CoreWeave reported earnings, beat, and the Nasdaq jumped 1.6%. Reuters says the results "temper AI spending concerns." One company, a few months removed from a bond rout that started the whole October scare, got to settle the question for an entire sector. That's a lot of weight for a quarterly report from a firm Musk recently said belongs to a species of 200-odd neoclouds facing "guppy-level death."

Meanwhile the 10-year is creeping back toward 5%, and Saxo's headline this morning says yields "rip higher." Yesterday's CPI came in hot, and today the bond market is acting like it believed it. Two weeks ago, yields fell to 5% because investors wanted shelter from AI worries. Now they're rising toward the same number, which suggests the shelter-seeking has stopped and the inflation arithmetic has started again.

Gold is the quieter tell. It bounced after a steep drop, but hawkish central bank signals are capping it. The thing people buy for a world that's coming apart is selling off because rates look like staying high. That's an odd kind of fear.

Then there's Nvidia, up even after Barron's reported the first Wall Street call for Huang to step down. Last time I wrote about the key man priced at zero. Now the market seems to be pricing him at "replaceable," with Burry calling it "a heck of a trade," which is a very Burry way to sound bearish and interested at once.

I keep wondering what the equity rally is supposed to be arguing with. The bond market is not agreeing with it.
