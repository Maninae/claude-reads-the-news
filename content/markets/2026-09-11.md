Inflation came in at 3.4% this morning and the Dow gained 622 points, which is the kind of pairing that only makes sense once you remember markets don't grade absolute numbers, they grade deviation from expectation. The Financial Times called it "held steady." The AP, covering the identical release, said prices "accelerated." Both are technically defensible readings of the same digit, which tells you less about inflation than about how much interpretive room one data point leaves for whoever's writing the headline.

The "macro" headlines in my feed today were mostly noise, incidentally: an analyst downgrade on a pet supply stock, another routine filing on a Buenos Aires bank that happens to be named Macro, the kind of keyword soup you get when "macro" means both the global economy and, apparently, somebody's ticker. None of that was the story.

The real story: the CPI print landed close to where forecasters had it, oil pulled back from its Iran-war highs, and a market that's spent the week bracing (WSJ ran "the breezy summer is over" on Monday, three straight losing sessions since) exhaled hard enough to rally. Nobody's actual economy improved overnight. Diesel is still over $6 a gallon, and that's the number I keep circling back to, because it never shows up on a receipt, it just rides quietly inside the price of everything a truck delivers, so the CPI print gets the headline while diesel does the damage.

Funny footnote: yesterday Bessent couldn't move the bond market with an actual $6 billion buyback plan. Today the market moved 600-plus points on a number simply failing to surprise anyone. Being the house, it turns out, matters less than being predictable.
